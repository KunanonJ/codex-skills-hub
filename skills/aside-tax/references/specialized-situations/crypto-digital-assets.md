# Crypto and Digital Assets

## Reporting overview

Digital asset sales usually belong on Form 8949 and then Schedule D. For 2025, Form 8949 uses dedicated digital-asset boxes instead of the traditional A-F set.

## Box selection on Form 8949

### Short-term

| Box | Typical use |
|---|---|
| G | Basis reported and no adjustment |
| H | Basis reported with adjustment |
| I | Basis not reported |

### Long-term

| Box | Typical use |
|---|---|
| J | Basis reported and no adjustment |
| K | Basis reported with adjustment |
| L | Basis not reported |

## Common categories

| Activity | Typical treatment |
|---|---|
| Sale or exchange of tokens or coins | Capital gain or loss |
| Staking rewards | Ordinary income when received |
| Mining income | Ordinary income; potentially business income |
| Airdrops and hard forks | Ordinary income when dominion and control exists |
| NFT sales | Often capital gain or loss, unless inventory or business facts change the character |

## Watchpoints

- Basis tracking can differ by lot-identification method.
- Some broker statements may show wash-sale style adjustments even though crypto wash-sale treatment remains unsettled.
- Capital gains from digital assets can also matter for NIIT.
- The NIIT treatment of staking rewards is still unsettled; `irs-instructions/f8960/` should be read closely when that issue matters.
