# Form 8833

`Form 8833` is not the default treaty form for every J-1 or nonresident treaty claim.

Use it when the taxpayer is taking a treaty-based return position that requires separate disclosure.

## Common J-1 use case

- The clearest J-1 scenario is a dual-resident tie-breaker claim.
- That means the person otherwise became a U.S. resident under the substantial presence test, but claims residence in the treaty country under the treaty tie-breaker rules.
- In that situation, the IRS J-1 guidance points to `Form 8833`.

## Cases that often do not start with Form 8833

- A nonresident claiming reduced wage withholding under a treaty usually starts with `Form 8233`.
- A nonresident claiming exempt scholarship or fellowship withholding often starts with `Form W-8BEN`.
- A nonresident filing the annual return still uses `Form 1040-NR` and Schedule OI even when a treaty article is relevant.

## Practical screen

Ask these questions in order:

1. Is this only a withholding-stage treaty claim?
2. Is the taxpayer still a nonresident alien, or already a resident under substantial presence?
3. Is the taxpayer taking a treaty position that overrides ordinary Code residency or otherwise needs treaty-based return-position disclosure?

If the answer to question 3 is yes, `Form 8833` moves to the front of the checklist.
