# Treaty Benefits for J-1 and Other Nonresident Filers

Start here when the taxpayer is from a country that has a U.S. income tax treaty.

## Core rule

Treaty benefits are controlled by the exact treaty text, not by visa type alone.

Check all of these:

1. The taxpayer's country has an income tax treaty with the United States.
2. The treaty has a relevant article for students, trainees, teachers, researchers, dependent personal services, or other compensation.
3. The taxpayer still qualifies under the treaty's time limit, saving clause, and any dollar cap or employer requirements.

## Withholding-stage claims

- Use `Form 8233` for treaty exemption or reduced withholding on compensation for personal services.
- `Form 8233` is given to the withholding agent, not filed as the annual income tax return.
- It is generally renewed each year the withholding claim continues.
- For noncompensatory scholarship or fellowship amounts and other non-service FDAP income, the IRS materials point instead to `Form W-8BEN`.

## Return-stage claims

- Report the income on `Form 1040-NR` as required by the treaty and the form instructions.
- Use Schedule OI on `Form 1040-NR` to disclose treaty country, article, and exempt amount.
- If treaty withholding was not fully handled upstream, reconcile it on the annual return.

## When `Form 8833` becomes relevant

- `Form 8833` is for treaty-based return positions that must be separately disclosed.
- The high-value J-1 example is a dual-resident tie-breaker claim after the person otherwise became a U.S. resident under the substantial presence test.
- Do not assume every treaty claim needs `Form 8833`; some compensation and scholarship claims are handled through the usual withholding forms and `Form 1040-NR` disclosures instead.
- See `references/nonresident-aliens/form-8833.md` for the local `8833` decision note.

## Practical workflow

1. Determine whether the taxpayer is still nonresident or has become resident under the substantial presence test.
2. Pull the treaty article from `Publication 901` and the actual treaty text.
3. Confirm time limits, saving-clause exceptions, and article-specific conditions.
4. Separate withholding forms (`8233`, `W-8BEN`) from annual-return forms (`1040-NR`, `8843`, sometimes `8833`).
