### Net Earnings From Self-Employment

In most cases, net earnings include your net profit from a farm or nonfarm business.

#### Partnership Income or Loss

When you were a general or limited partner in a partnership, you must include earnings from box 14, code A, of Schedule K-1 (Form 1065) on either line 1a or line 2. General partners should reduce this amount by certain expenses before entering it on Schedule SE, requiring an attached explanation if reductions are made.

Limited partners should include only guaranteed payments for services actually rendered to or on behalf of the partnership. The determination of limited partner status depends on whether the partner qualifies under section 1402(a)(13).

**Special circumstances.** If a partner died and the partnership continued, include the deceased's distributive share through the end of the month death occurred. Married couples who were both partners in a partnership must each report their net earnings from self-employment separately on individual Schedule SE forms.

**Community income consideration.** Your own distributive share of partnership income is included in figuring your net earnings from self-employment. Unlike income tax divisions between spouses, no part of your share can be included in your spouse's net earnings from self-employment.

#### Other Income and Losses Included in Net Earnings From Self-Employment

The following categories are included:

1. **Farm rental income** when you materially participated in production or management of farm products on the land (material participation tests explained in Pub. 225).

2. **Department of Agriculture payments** for subsidy or conservation reserve program participation.

3. **Payments for rooms or space** when you provided substantial tenant services (hotels, boarding houses, tourist camps, trailer parks, warehouses, storage garages).

4. **Newspaper/magazine retail sales** if you were age 18 or older and kept the profits.

5. **Direct seller income** with specific conditions (detailed in Pub. 334).

6. **Insurance agent amounts** including:
   - Retirement payments calculated as percentage of pre-retirement commissions
   - Renewal commissions
   - Deferred commissions from pre-retirement sales

7. **Fishing vessel crew fees** for certain crew members on vessels with normally fewer than 10 people.

8. **Fee-basis government employee fees** when paid only on fee basis and job wasn't covered under federal-state social security agreement.

9. **Interest received** in the course of trade or business (notes, accounts receivable).

10. **Director fees** for corporation director services.

11. **Recapture amounts** under sections 179 and 280F when business use dropped to 50% or less (exclude disposition amounts; see Form 4797).

12. **Professional fiduciary fees** and nonprofessional fiduciary fees relating to active operation participation or extensive management activities.

13. **Section 1256 contract gains/losses** by options or commodities dealers in normal dealing activities.

#### Income and Losses Not Included in Net Earnings From Self-Employment

The following categories are excluded:

1. **Employee income** including salaries, fees, and other income subject to social security or Medicare tax for employee services (includes railroad retirement system and public official services except fee-basis government employees).

2. **Notary public fees** (check box 3 on Schedule 2 if this is your only income; if you have $400 or more of other SE tax earnings, enter the amount on the dotted line left of line 3).

3. **Retired partner payments** under written partnership plan providing lifelong periodic payments when you had no other partnership interest and performed no services.

4. **Real estate rental income** not received in course of trade or business as real estate dealer (report on Schedule E).

5. **Farm rentals** including crop-share rentals when you didn't materially participate in production or management (report on Form 4835; use two forms if you and spouse elected QJV treatment).

6. **CRP payments** if you're receiving social security benefits for retirement or disability (deduct on line 1b).

7. **Investment income** including stock dividends and bond interest issued with coupons or in registered form (exclude if received as dealer in stocks/securities).

8. **Capital gain or loss** from:
   - Sale or exchange of capital assets
   - Sale, exchange, involuntary conversion of property not held for inventory or customer sale
   - Timber, coal, or domestic iron ore transactions

9. **Net operating losses** from other years.

10. **Termination payments to former insurance salespersons** meeting all of the following conditions:
    - Received from insurance company for prior services
    - Received after agreement termination
    - No services performed after termination through year-end
    - Covenant not to compete for at least 1 year from termination date
    - Payment amount depends on policies sold/credited during final year or policy persistence
    - Payment amount doesn't depend on length of service or overall earnings

### Additional Medicare Tax

A 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax applies when Schedule SE line 6 totals exceed the following threshold amounts based on filing status:

| Filing Status | Threshold Amount |
|---|---|
| Married filing jointly | $250,000 |
| Married filing separately | $125,000 |
| Single | $200,000 |
| Head of household | $200,000 |
| Qualifying surviving spouse | $200,000 |

If you have both wages and self-employment income, the threshold amount for applying the Additional Medicare Tax on the self-employment income is reduced (but not below zero) by the amount of wages subject to Additional Medicare Tax.

Use Form 8959, Additional Medicare Tax, to figure this tax.
