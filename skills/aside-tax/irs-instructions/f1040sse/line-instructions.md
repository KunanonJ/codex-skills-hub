### Specific Instructions

#### Part I -- Line Instructions

##### Line 1a

Enter on line 1a your net farm profit (or loss) from Schedule F, line 34, and your share of farm partnership net income (or loss) from Schedule K-1 (Form 1065), box 14, code A.

##### Line 1b

If you were receiving social security retirement or social security disability benefits at the time you received your Conservation Reserve Program (CRP) payment(s), enter the amount of your taxable CRP payment(s) on line 1b.

##### Line 2

Enter on line 2 your net profit (or loss) from nonfarm self-employment. This includes:

- Net profit (or loss) from Schedule C, line 31
- Your share of nonfarm partnership net income (or loss) from Schedule K-1 (Form 1065), box 14, code A
- Income from services performed as a minister, member of a religious order, or Christian Science practitioner (see Ministers, Members of Religious Orders, and Christian Science Practitioners, earlier)
- Income as a U.S. citizen employed by a foreign government or international organization for services in the United States (see earlier)

##### Line 3

Combine lines 1a, 1b, and 2 and enter the result on line 3. If you need to adjust for community income or Chapter 11 bankruptcy income, see the earlier instructions under Community Income or Chapter 11 Bankruptcy Cases, and enter the notation and amount on the dotted line to the left of line 3.

##### Lines 4a Through 4c

**Line 4a.** If line 3 is more than zero, multiply line 3 by 92.35% (0.9235). Enter the result on line 4a. This is your net earnings from self-employment before any optional methods.

**Line 4b.** If you had church employee income, enter on line 4b the amount from Form W-2, Wage and Tax Statement. Church employee income is wages you received as an employee (other than as a minister, member of a religious order, or Christian Science practitioner) of a church or qualified church-controlled organization that has a certificate in effect electing an exemption from employer social security and Medicare taxes.

**Line 4c.** Add lines 4a and 4b. Enter the result on line 4c.

- If line 4c is less than $400 and you don't have church employee income of $108.28 or more, you don't owe SE tax. But if the total of lines 1a and 2 is $434 or more, file Schedule SE (completed through line 4c) with your tax return.
- If line 4c is $400 or more or you had church employee income of $108.28 or more, continue to line 5a.

##### Line 5a

Enter on line 5a any church employee income from Form W-2. See Employees of Churches and Church Organizations, earlier.

##### Line 5b

Multiply line 5a by 92.35% (0.9235). Enter the result on line 5b.

##### Line 6

Enter the amount of net earnings from self-employment. If you did not use the optional methods, this is the amount from line 4a plus line 5b. If you used the optional methods in Part II, follow the instructions on the form.

##### Line 7

Enter the maximum amount of combined wages, tips, and net earnings subject to Social Security tax. For 2025, this amount is $176,100.

##### Line 8a

Enter the total social security wages and tips from Form(s) W-2 (boxes 3 and 7). Include unreported tips subject to social security tax from Form 4137, line 10.

##### Line 8b

Enter any unreported tips subject to social security tax from Form 4137, line 10 (if not already included on line 8a).

##### Line 9

Subtract line 8a from line 7. If zero or less, enter -0- on line 9 and go to line 10.

##### Line 10

Multiply the smaller of line 6 or line 9 by 12.4% (0.124). Enter the result on line 10. This is the Social Security tax portion.

##### Line 11

Multiply line 6 by 2.9% (0.029). Enter the result on line 11. This is the Medicare tax portion.

##### Line 12

Add lines 10 and 11. Enter the result on line 12. This is your total self-employment tax.

##### Line 13

Multiply line 12 by 50% (0.50). Enter the result on line 13. This is your deduction for the employer-equivalent portion of self-employment tax. Enter this amount on Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 15.

If you are filing Form 1040-SS, skip line 13.

#### Part II -- Optional Methods

You can use the optional methods to figure your net earnings from self-employment only if you meet certain requirements. Using an optional method may give you credit toward your social security coverage even though you have a loss or a small amount of income from self-employment.

**How the Optional Methods Can Help You.** The optional methods may provide several benefits:

- **Social Security Coverage:** The optional methods may give you credit toward your social security coverage even though you have a loss or a small amount of income from self-employment.
- **Credits and Deductions:** Using optional methods could qualify you for the earned income credit (EIC), additional child tax credit (ACTC), or child and dependent care credit, particularly if net earnings are below $7,240. Figure the EIC, ACTC, and child and dependent care credit with and without using the optional methods.
- **AGI Impact:** Using the optional methods may decrease your AGI, which may affect your eligibility for credits, deductions, or other items that are subject to an AGI limit.

**Important:** Using the optional methods as described above may be beneficial, but they may also increase your SE tax.

**Changing Your Method.** You may amend your election after filing by using Form 1040-X to switch between regular and optional methods.

##### Farm Optional Method

**Eligibility requirements:**

You can use the farm optional method if:

- Your gross farm income was $10,860 or less, **or**
- Your net farm profits were less than $7,840.

**Calculation:** Report in Part II, line 15, two-thirds of your gross farm income, up to $7,240, as your net earnings.

**Key features:**

- There is no limit on how many years you can use this method.
- Can increase or decrease net earnings even if farming had a loss.
- For partnerships: figure your share of gross income based on the partnership agreement.

**Partnership-specific rules:** If you received guaranteed payments, your share equals guaranteed payments plus your share of remaining gross income. Limited partners include only guaranteed payments for services rendered.

##### Nonfarm Optional Method

**Eligibility requirements:**

You can use the nonfarm optional method only if all of the following apply:

- Your net nonfarm profits were less than $7,840, **and**
- Your net nonfarm profits were less than 72.189% of your gross nonfarm income, **and**
- You were "regularly self-employed" (you had net earnings of $400 or more from self-employment in at least 2 of the 3 prior consecutive tax years).

**Limitation:** You can use the nonfarm optional method to figure your earnings from self-employment for only 5 years. The 5 years don't have to be consecutive.

**Calculation:** Report in Part II, line 17, two-thirds of your gross nonfarm income, up to the amount on line 16, as your net earnings. But you can't report less than your actual net earnings from nonfarm self-employment.

**Income sources:** Includes Schedule C, line 31 and Schedule K-1 (Form 1065) nonfarm partnerships.

##### Using Both Optional Methods

If you can use both methods, you can report less than your total actual net earnings from farm and nonfarm self-employment, but you can't report less than your actual net earnings from nonfarm self-employment alone.

If you use both methods to figure net earnings, you can't report more than $7,240 of net earnings from self-employment.
