# Form 8995-A Schedule C: Loss Netting and Carryforward

## Purpose

Schedule C nets QBI losses against QBI income across all qualified trades or businesses and tracks QBI loss carryforwards from prior years.

## When to Complete

Complete Schedule C if you have:
- A qualified trade or business with a QBI **loss** (negative QBI on line 2 of Form 8995-A), or
- A QBI loss carryforward from a prior tax year

## Loss Netting Rules

1. **Net current-year losses against current-year income:** If some businesses have positive QBI and others have negative QBI, the losses offset the income.

2. **Apply prior-year loss carryforward:** After netting current-year amounts, apply any prior-year QBI loss carryforward. The carryforward reduces positive QBI dollar-for-dollar.

3. **If net QBI is positive:** Allocate the remaining net QBI proportionally among the businesses that had positive QBI. Each business's allocated QBI then goes through the W-2 wage/UBIA limitation in Part II.

4. **If net QBI is zero or negative:** The QBI component of the deduction is $0 for the current year. The net loss carries forward to the next tax year.

## Proportional Allocation

When losses reduce total positive QBI, the remaining QBI is allocated proportionally:

**Reduction ratio** = Net positive QBI ÷ Total positive QBI (before netting)

For each positive-QBI business:
- **Allocated QBI** = Original positive QBI × Reduction ratio

## Loss Carryforward

- Unused QBI losses carry forward indefinitely
- Losses are applied in the order they arise (FIFO)
- The carryforward reduces QBI **before** applying the W-2 wage/UBIA limitation
- Track the carryforward on Schedule C and enter on Form 8995-A Part IV, line 29 for REIT/PTP losses

## Example

Three businesses:
- Business A: QBI = $80,000
- Business B: QBI = −$30,000
- Prior-year loss carryforward: $10,000

1. Net current year: $80,000 + (−$30,000) = $50,000
2. Apply carryforward: $50,000 − $10,000 = $40,000
3. Reduction ratio: $40,000 ÷ $80,000 = 0.50
4. Business A allocated QBI: $80,000 × 0.50 = $40,000
5. Business A's $40,000 then goes through the W-2 wage/UBIA limitation in Part II
