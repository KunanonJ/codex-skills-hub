# Form 8995-A: General Instructions

## Qualified Business Income (QBI)

QBI is the net amount of qualified items of income, gain, deduction, and loss from any qualified trade or business. Only items included in taxable income are counted.

QBI includes:
- Schedule C net profit (after all business expenses)
- Partnership/S corporation K-1 QBI items
- Rental income (if it rises to the level of a trade or business)

QBI does **not** include:
- Capital gains or losses
- Interest income not properly allocable to a trade or business
- Wage income (W-2 wages received as an employee)
- Guaranteed payments for services from a partnership
- Reasonable compensation paid by an S corporation
- Items not effectively connected with the conduct of a trade or business within the U.S.

**Important:** Before computing QBI for a Schedule C business, subtract the deductible portion of SE tax (Schedule SE line 13 → Schedule 1 line 15) and the self-employed health insurance deduction (Schedule 1 line 17) that are allocable to that business. These reduce QBI.

## Specified Service Trade or Business (SSTB)

An SSTB is a trade or business involving the performance of services in the fields of:
- Health (doctors, dentists, nurses, etc.)
- Law
- Accounting
- Actuarial science
- Performing arts
- Consulting
- Athletics
- Financial services
- Brokerage services
- Any trade or business where the principal asset is the reputation or skill of one or more of its employees or owners

**Exceptions:** Engineering and architecture are specifically **excluded** from the SSTB definition.

### SSTB Income Limitations

- **Below threshold:** SSTBs qualify for QBI deduction just like any other business — no limitation.
- **In phase-in range:** SSTB QBI, W-2 wages, and UBIA are reduced by the applicable percentage. Complete Schedule A (SSTB) to compute the reduction.
- **Above phase-in range:** SSTB gets **zero** QBI deduction — fully excluded.

## W-2 Wages

W-2 wages are the total wages subject to wage withholding, elective deferrals, and deferred compensation paid by the qualified trade or business with respect to employment of its employees during the tax year.

For sole proprietors (Schedule C): If you have no employees, your W-2 wages are $0. The owner's own drawings are not W-2 wages.

For partnerships and S corporations: W-2 wages are reported on Schedule K-1 or the entity's supporting statements.

## UBIA of Qualified Property

UBIA (Unadjusted Basis Immediately After Acquisition) is the cost basis of tangible, depreciable property held by the qualified trade or business at the close of the tax year, and that has not reached the end of its depreciable period.

The depreciable period is the later of:
- 10 years after the property is placed in service, or
- The last day of the last full year in the applicable recovery period under MACRS

## Aggregation of Businesses (Schedule B)

Taxpayers may elect to aggregate multiple trades or businesses if they meet **all** of the following requirements:
1. The same person or group of persons owns 50% or more of each trade or business to be aggregated, directly or indirectly
2. The ownership exists for a majority of the tax year
3. All items attributable to each trade or business are reported on returns with the same tax year
4. None of the trades or businesses is an SSTB
5. The trades or businesses satisfy at least **two** of these three factors:
   a. They provide products and services that are the same or customarily offered together
   b. They share facilities or significant centralized business elements
   c. They are operated in coordination with, or reliance upon, other businesses in the aggregated group

**Once made, the aggregation election applies for all subsequent tax years** unless there is a significant change in facts and circumstances.

When businesses are aggregated, their QBI, W-2 wages, and UBIA are combined for purposes of applying the W-2 wage/UBIA limitation. This can benefit taxpayers by allowing a high-wage business to "share" its wages with a low-wage business.

## Loss Netting (Schedule C)

When a taxpayer has both positive-QBI and negative-QBI businesses:
1. Net the positive QBI against the negative QBI across all businesses
2. Apply any prior-year QBI loss carryforward
3. If the net is positive, allocate the remaining QBI proportionally among the positive-QBI businesses
4. If the net is zero or negative, the QBI component of the deduction is $0 and the loss carries forward to the next year

The loss carryforward reduces QBI in subsequent years **before** applying the W-2 wage/UBIA limitation.
