### Part I -- HSA Contributions and Deductions

Use Part I to figure your HSA deduction, excess contributions you make, and excess employer contributions.

#### Figuring Your HSA Deduction

For 2025, the maximum contribution amounts are:

| Coverage Type | Maximum Contribution |
|---|---|
| Self-only | $4,300 |
| Family | $8,550 |

**Additional contribution for individuals age 55 or older:** $1,000 per year.

The maximum contribution is reduced by:
- Employer contributions to your HSA (included in line 9)
- Archer MSA contributions for the year
- Qualified HSA funding distributions from an IRA (included in line 10)

**You cannot deduct contributions:**
- For months you were enrolled in Medicare.
- If you can be claimed as another person's dependent for 2025.

#### How To Complete Part I

**Both spouses with HSAs.** If both you and your spouse each have an HSA:
- Complete lines 1 through 13 as instructed below.
- If either spouse has an HDHP with family coverage: Both spouses are treated as having only family coverage (disregard any self-only plan).
- File a separate Form 8889 for each spouse.
- Combine the line 13 amounts from both Forms 8889.
- Enter the combined amount on Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 13.
- Attach both Forms 8889 to the paper return.

#### Line 1 -- Coverage Type

Check the box indicating the type of coverage under your HDHP on the first day of the last month of your tax year (generally December 1). If you had coverage under an HDHP for less than the entire year, check the box for the type of plan that was in effect for the longer period.

Rules:
- If you were covered by both a self-only and family HDHP at the same time: You are treated as having family coverage during that period.
- If either spouse had an HDHP with family coverage: Both spouses are treated as having family coverage, regardless of whether you file jointly or separately.
- December 1 coverage determination: Check the appropriate box based on coverage on that date.

#### Line 2 -- Contributions You Made for 2025

Include:
- Amounts you or others (other than your employer) contributed to your HSA for 2025.
- Amounts contributed for 2025 in 2026 by the unextended due date of your return (April 15, 2026).
- If you served in a designated combat zone or contingency operation, you may have an extended time to make contributions. See Publication 3 for details.

Exclude:
- Employer contributions (report those on line 9).
- Rollovers from another HSA or Archer MSA.
- Qualified HSA funding distributions from an IRA (report those on line 10).
- Payroll contributions made through a salary reduction agreement under a cafeteria plan -- these are treated as employer contributions.

#### Line 3 -- HSA Deduction Limitation

Rules for determining your amount on line 3:

1. **Use family coverage amount** if you or your spouse had an HDHP with family coverage at any time during the year (disregard any self-only plan).

2. **Last-month rule application.** If the last-month rule applies, you are treated as an eligible individual for the entire year with the same HDHP coverage type as you had on December 1.

3. **Eligible for the entire year with no change in coverage type.** Enter $4,300 (self-only) or $8,550 (family).

4. **Eligible for the entire year with a change in coverage type during the year.** Enter the greater of:
   - The result from the Line 3 Limitation Chart and Worksheet, OR
   - The maximum based on coverage type on December 1.
   - **Tip:** If you had family coverage on December 1, enter $8,550 (no worksheet needed).

5. **Not eligible on December 1.** Use the Line 3 Limitation Chart and Worksheet.

6. **Age 55 or older at end of year:**
   - If unmarried or married with self-only coverage the entire year: Add $1,000 to the amount from rule (3) or (4) above.
   - The $1,000 additional contribution is NOT allocable among spouses (unlike the $8,550 family contribution limit).
   - For the worksheet: The additional amount is included for each month you are eligible.
   - **Note:** If married and either spouse had family coverage at any time, figure the additional contribution on line 7, NOT on line 3.

**Tip:** If your eligibility and coverage type did not change from month to month, enter the same number in the worksheet for each month.

##### Line 3 Limitation Chart and Worksheet

**Flowchart for Determining Monthly Contribution Limitation:**

**Decision (1): Were you enrolled in Medicare for the month?**
- YES: Enter $0 for the month. Go to the next month.
- NO: Go to Decision (2).

**Decision (2): Were you an eligible individual on the 1st day of the month?**
- YES: Go to Decision (3).
- NO: Enter $0 for the month. Go to the next month.

**Decision (3): What type of HDHP coverage did you have on the 1st day of the month?**

**Self-only coverage:**
- Enter $4,300 for the month.
- If you are age 55 or older at year-end: Enter $5,300 for the month.

**Family coverage:**
- Enter $8,550 for the month.
- If you are unmarried and age 55 or older at year-end: Enter $9,550 for the month.

#### Line 4

If your spouse also has a separate HSA and either of you had family HDHP coverage: Enter the same amount as line 3.

#### Line 5 -- Total Contribution Limit

Generally, if line 4 is blank, enter the amount from line 3.

If line 4 has an amount, add lines 3 and 7 (or use the applicable calculation). See the form instructions for the specific computation.

#### Line 6 -- Allocation Between Spouses

**Spouses with separate HSAs and family coverage:**

**If you had family coverage for all 12 months:**
- Divide line 5 equally between spouses (unless you agree on a different allocation).
- Enter your share on line 6.

**If you did NOT have family coverage for all 12 months:**

Step 1: Recalculate line 5 using only the worksheet months with family coverage. Use the same line 4 amount.

Step 2: Divide the Step 1 result equally between spouses (unless you agree on a different allocation).

Step 3: Subtract your spouse's allocated portion (Step 2) from the Step 1 result.

Step 4: Determine other contribution limits that apply for the tax year and add the result to Step 3. Enter the total on line 6.

#### Line 7 -- Additional Contribution Amount

Use the Additional Contribution Amount Worksheet if ALL of the following apply:
1. You are age 55 or older at the end of the year AND you are married.
2. You or your spouse had HDHP family coverage AND you were an eligible individual on the 1st day of the month.
3. You were NOT enrolled in Medicare for the month.

Enter the worksheet result on line 7.

**Tip:** If conditions (1) through (3) apply for all 12 months, enter $1,000.

For most filers: Enter the amount from line 5, or line 6 if applicable.

#### Line 9 -- Employer Contributions

Include:
- Amounts your employer contributed to your HSA for 2025.
- Contributions made by your health insurance plan on your employer's behalf.
- This amount should appear on your Form W-2, box 12, code W.

Complete the Employer Contribution Worksheet if:
- Employer contributions for 2024 appear on your 2025 Form W-2, box 12, code W.

#### Line 10 -- Qualified HSA Funding Distribution

A qualified HSA funding distribution is a distribution from a traditional IRA or Roth IRA made via direct trustee-to-trustee transfer to your HSA.

Key points:
- The distribution is NOT included in income.
- The distribution is NOT deductible.
- It reduces your maximum HSA contribution amount.
- You cannot make a qualified HSA funding distribution from an ongoing SEP IRA or SIMPLE IRA. An ongoing SEP IRA or SIMPLE IRA is one for which an employer contribution is made for the plan year ending with or within the tax year.
- The maximum exclusion from income is based on your age at year-end and HDHP coverage type (self-only or family) at the time of the distribution.
- **You may only make ONE qualified HSA funding distribution during your lifetime.**
- **Exception:** If the distribution was made during a month you had self-only HDHP coverage, you can make one additional distribution in a later month of the same year if you change to family coverage.

See the line 13 instructions for treatment of excess contributions. See Publication 969 for additional information.

##### Testing Period for Qualified HSA Funding Distribution

- Begins: The month the IRA distribution is contributed to the HSA.
- Lasts: Through the last day of the 12th month following that month.
- Example: A June 17, 2025 distribution creates a testing period through June 30, 2026.
- If you fail to remain an eligible individual during the testing period (other than by reason of death or disability): Include the qualified HSA funding distribution in income for the year you fail to be eligible, plus a 10% additional tax (see Part III).

#### Line 11

For most filers: Enter the same amount as line 10.

#### Line 12 -- Contribution Limitation

Generally: Line 8 minus lines 9 and 10 (and line 11 if applicable).

#### Line 13 -- Your HSA Deduction

Generally: Enter the SMALLER of line 2 or line 12 on line 13 and on Schedule 1 (Form 1040), Part II, line 13.

**If line 2 is more than line 13:** You, your employer, or someone else contributed MORE than the allowable amount. You may owe additional tax on the excess contributions. See Excess Contributions below.

**If line 2 is less than line 12 and you have prior year excess contributions:** See Prior Year Excess Contributions below.

##### Excess Contributions You Make

Figure the excess: Subtract your deductible contributions (line 13) from your actual contributions (line 2).

**You can withdraw excess contributions without penalty if:**
1. You withdraw the excess by the due date (including extensions) of your 2025 tax return.
2. You do NOT claim a deduction for the withdrawn amount.
3. You also withdraw any earnings on the withdrawn contributions.
4. You include the earnings as "Other income" on your tax return for the year of withdrawal.

##### Excess Employer Contributions

Excess employer contributions equal the employer contributions that exceed your contribution limitation (line 8).

If you made a qualified HSA funding distribution: Reduce line 8 by that distribution amount before determining the excess.

**If the excess is not included in income on Form W-2:** Report the excess as "Other income" on your tax return.

**You can withdraw the excess if:**
1. You withdraw the excess by the due date (including extensions) of your 2025 tax return.
2. You do NOT claim an exclusion from income for the withdrawn amount.
3. You also withdraw any earnings on the withdrawn contributions.
4. You include the earnings as "Other income" on your tax return for the year of withdrawal.

##### Important Note on Late Withdrawal

If you timely filed your return without withdrawing the excess:
- You can still withdraw the excess within 6 months after the due date of the tax return (excluding extensions).
- File an amended return with "Filed pursuant to section 301.9100-2" written at the top.
- Include an explanation of the withdrawal.
- Make all necessary changes on the amended return (e.g., file an amended Form 5329 if you reported the excess on the original return).

##### Prior Year Excess Contributions

If line 2 is less than line 12 and you have prior year excess contributions: See the Instructions for Form 5329, Part VII, line 43.

Any excess contribution remaining in the HSA at the end of the year is subject to the 6% additional tax. See Form 5329 and its instructions.
