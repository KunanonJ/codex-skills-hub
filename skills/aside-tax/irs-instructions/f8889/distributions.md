### Part II -- HSA Distributions (If you are not filing jointly and you have more than one HSA, see the instructions above under Death of Account Beneficiary -- Multiple HSAs.)

#### Line 14a -- Total Distributions From All HSAs

Enter the total distributions received from all your HSAs in 2025. This amount should appear on Form 1099-SA, box 1.

#### Line 14b -- Distributions Rolled Over or Excess Contributions Withdrawn

Include on line 14b:
- Distributions that qualify as a rollover contribution to another HSA (see Rollovers definition above).
- Excess contributions (and earnings on those excess contributions) withdrawn by the due date (including extensions) of your return (see line 13 instructions above).

#### Line 15 -- Qualified Medical Expenses Paid Using HSA Distributions

**Caution:** Only include distributions that were used for qualified medical expenses that were NOT reimbursed by insurance or other coverage, and that were incurred AFTER your HSA was established. Do NOT include excess contribution distributions taken after the due date (including extensions) even if they were used for qualified medical expenses.

Include distributions from ALL your HSAs in 2025 that were used for qualified medical expenses of:
1. You and your spouse.
2. All your dependents you claim on your return.
3. Any person you could have claimed as a dependent except that:
   - The person filed a joint return,
   - The person had gross income of $5,050 or more, or
   - You (or your spouse, if filing jointly) could be claimed as a dependent on someone else's return.

**Tip:** A child of divorced, separated, or living-apart parents (for more than 6 months of the calendar year) is treated as a dependent of both parents for this purpose, whether or not the custodial parent releases the dependent claim.

**Caution:** You cannot deduct on Schedule A (Form 1040) any qualified medical expenses that you include on line 15.

#### Line 16 -- Taxable HSA Distributions

Calculate: Line 14a minus lines 14b and 15. This is the portion of your HSA distributions included in income.

#### Lines 17a and 17b -- Additional 20% Tax on HSA Distributions

Distributions included in income from line 16 are generally subject to an additional 20% tax unless an exception applies.

##### Exceptions to the Additional 20% Tax

The additional 20% tax does NOT apply to distributions made after the account beneficiary:
- Dies
- Becomes disabled (within the meaning of section 72(m)(7))
- Turns age 65

**Line 17a.** Check the box on line 17a if any of the above exceptions apply to any of the distributions included on line 16.

**Line 17b.** Enter 20% (0.20) multiplied by the amount on line 16 that does NOT qualify for any of the exceptions listed above.

**Note:** In limited unusual circumstances involving mistaken distributions, the additional tax may not apply. See Notice 2004-50, Q&A 37 and 76, at IRS.gov/IRB/2004-33_IRB#NOT-2004-50.

### Part III -- Income and Additional Tax for Failure To Maintain HDHP Coverage

Use Part III to figure additional income and adjustments for Schedule 1 (Form 1040) and additional taxes for Schedule 2 (Form 1040) if you failed to maintain eligible individual status during the testing period for:

- The last-month rule (see the definition of Last-Month Rule above).
- A qualified HSA funding distribution (see the Line 10 instructions above).

Include the amount in income for the year you fail to be an eligible individual.

#### Line 18 -- Last-Month Rule

Use the Line 3 Limitation Chart and Worksheet for the year the contribution was made to determine the contribution you could have made WITHOUT the last-month rule.

Enter on line 18: The excess of the amount contributed over the redetermined amount (the amount you could have contributed without the last-month rule).

#### Line 19 -- Qualified HSA Funding Distribution

Enter the total of any qualified HSA funding distribution included on line 10 for which you failed to remain an eligible individual during the testing period.

#### Line 20 -- Total Income

Add lines 18 and 19. Include this amount in income on Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 8f.

#### Line 21 -- Additional Tax

Multiply line 20 by 10% (0.10). Include this amount on Schedule 2 (Form 1040), line 17d.
