### General Instructions

#### Purpose of Form

Use Form 8889 to:
- Report HSA contributions (including those made on your behalf and employer contributions).
- Calculate your HSA deduction.
- Report distributions from HSAs.
- Calculate income and additional tax for failure to maintain eligible individual status.

**Additional Information.** See Publication 969 (Health Savings Accounts and Other Tax-Favored Health Plans), the Instructions for Form 1040, and the Instructions for Form 1040-NR for more information.

#### Who Must File

You must file Form 8889 if any of the following apply:
- You, your employer, or another person contributed to your HSA (or you wish to take an HSA deduction).
- You received HSA distributions in 2025.
- You failed to maintain "eligible individual" status during the testing period.
- You acquired an interest in an HSA because of the death of the account beneficiary.

**Important.** If you received HSA distributions in 2025, you must file Form 8889 with Form 1040, Form 1040-SR, or Form 1040-NR, even if you have no taxable income or other filing requirement.

### Definitions

#### Eligible Individual

To be an eligible individual and qualify for HSA contributions, you must meet ALL of the following requirements:
- You are covered under a high deductible health plan (HDHP).
- You have no other health coverage except disregarded coverage (see below).
- You are not enrolled in Medicare.
- You cannot be claimed as another person's dependent for 2025.
- You cannot fail to be eligible solely due to receiving VA hospital care for a service-connected disability.

You must be an eligible individual on the 1st day of a month to deduct contributions for that month.

##### Last-Month Rule

You may consider yourself an "eligible individual" for the entire year if you are an eligible individual on December 1 (the last day of the last month of the tax year). This is subject to the testing period requirements below.

##### Testing Period

- Begins: Last month of tax year (December 2025).
- Ends: Last day of the 12th month following that month (December 31, 2026).
- If you fail to remain an eligible individual during the testing period (other than by reason of death or disability): You must include in income the total contributions that would not have been made but for the last-month rule, plus a 10% additional tax.

##### Certain Coverage Disregarded

You are still considered an eligible individual even if you have:
1. Coverage for permitted insurance benefits.
2. Coverage for accidents, disability, dental care, vision care, long-term care, or telehealth and remote care services.

##### Permitted Insurance

Permitted insurance includes:
- Workers' compensation
- Disability benefits
- Automobile liability insurance
- Homeowners/renters insurance
- Other liability insurance

#### Account Beneficiary

The individual for whom the HSA was established.

#### HSA

A health savings account established exclusively for paying qualified medical expenses of the account beneficiary, the beneficiary's spouse, or dependents.

#### Distributions From an HSA

Distributions used exclusively for qualified medical expenses of the account beneficiary, spouse, or dependents are excludable from gross income. Distributions not used for qualified medical expenses are includible in gross income and subject to an additional 20% tax (with exceptions -- see Part II instructions).

#### Qualified Medical Expenses

Generally, qualified medical expenses are unreimbursed medical expenses that could be deducted on Schedule A (Form 1040). Qualified medical expenses include:

**Covered items:**
- Medical care for yourself, your spouse, and your dependents
- Nonprescription medicines (other than insulin)
- Menstrual care products (tampons, pads, liners, cups, sponges, and similar products)
- Condoms (as qualified medical expenses)
- Personal protective equipment for COVID-19 prevention (face masks, hand sanitizer, sanitizing wipes)
- Home COVID-19 testing kits
- Nutrition, wellness, and general health expenses (see IRS.gov FAQs)

**Expenses incurred before the HSA was established do not qualify.**

**Insurance Premiums.** Generally, you cannot treat insurance premiums as qualified medical expenses. However, exceptions apply for:

1. **Long-term care (LTC) insurance.** Premiums for qualified long-term care insurance contracts, subject to age-based limits.
2. **Health care continuation coverage (COBRA).** Premiums paid for COBRA continuation coverage.
3. **Health care coverage while receiving unemployment compensation.** Health insurance premiums paid while receiving unemployment compensation under federal or state law.
4. **Medicare and other health care coverage if age 65 or older.** Premiums for Medicare Part A or B, Medicare HMO, and the employee share of premiums for employer-sponsored health insurance. This does NOT include Medigap premiums.

Note: For exceptions (2) and (3), coverage may be for the account beneficiary, spouse, or dependent. For exception (4), if the account beneficiary is under age 65, premiums for a spouse's or dependent's Medicare coverage (if the spouse or dependent is age 65 or older) generally do NOT qualify.

#### High Deductible Health Plan (HDHP)

For 2025, an HDHP is a health plan with the following minimum deductibles and maximum out-of-pocket expenses:

| Coverage Type | Minimum Annual Deductible | Maximum Annual Out-of-Pocket |
|---|---|---|
| Self-only | $1,650 | $8,300 |
| Family | $3,300 | $16,600 |

The out-of-pocket limit excludes deductibles and expenses for out-of-network services if the plan uses a network of providers. Only the deductible and out-of-pocket expenses for services within the network (such as copayments and other amounts, but not premiums) are used to figure whether the limit applies.

##### Safe Harbor: Insulin Products

A plan will NOT fail to be an HDHP merely because it does not have a deductible for selected insulin products. This safe harbor applies regardless of whether the insulin is used for diabetes diagnosis or prevention.

##### Safe Harbor: Preventive Care Deductible

A plan will NOT fail to be an HDHP merely because it does not have a deductible for preventive care services.

##### Safe Harbor: Telehealth Deductible

A plan will NOT fail to be an HDHP merely because it does not have a deductible for telehealth and remote care services.

##### Special Rule: Surprise Billing

A plan will NOT fail to be an HDHP merely because it provides benefits required under the surprise billing provisions of federal law before satisfaction of the plan deductible.

#### Death of Account Beneficiary

**If the surviving spouse is the designated beneficiary:**
- The HSA is treated as the surviving spouse's own HSA.
- The surviving spouse completes Form 8889 as the account beneficiary.

**If the designated beneficiary is NOT the surviving spouse (or there is no designated beneficiary):**
- The account ceases to be an HSA as of the date of death.
- The beneficiary must complete Form 8889 as follows:
  - Write "Death of HSA account beneficiary" across the top of the form.
  - Skip Part I.
  - Line 14a: Enter the fair market value of the HSA as of the date of death.
  - Line 15: Enter qualified medical expenses of the deceased incurred before death and paid by the beneficiary within 1 year after the date of death.
  - Complete the remainder of Part II.
  - The distribution is NOT subject to the additional 20% tax.
  - Report any post-death earnings on the HSA as income.

**If the account beneficiary's estate is the beneficiary:**
- Include the fair market value of the HSA on the beneficiary's final return.
- Complete Form 8889 including Part I if applicable.

**Multiple HSAs.** If you are the beneficiary of 2 or more HSAs, or if you have your own HSA plus you are a beneficiary of another person's HSA, file a separate Form 8889 for each HSA. Mark additional Forms 8889 as "statement" at the top. Complete one controlling Form 8889 combining the amounts from each statement form. Attach statement forms to the paper return after the controlling Form 8889.

#### Deemed Distributions From HSAs

The following result in deemed distributions (the account ceases to be an HSA or the amount must be included in income):

1. **Prohibited transaction** under IRC section 4975: The account ceases to be an HSA as of January 1, 2025. Include the fair market value of all assets as of January 1, 2025, on line 14a.
2. **Used as security for a loan**: Include the fair market value of the assets used as security as income on Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 8f.

Deemed distributions are not treated as used for qualified medical expenses. They are generally subject to the additional 20% tax.

#### Rollovers

A rollover is a tax-free distribution from one HSA or Archer MSA that is reinvested in another HSA of the same account beneficiary.
- The rollover must be completed within 60 days of receiving the distribution.
- Only one rollover is permitted per 12-month period per account.
- Direct trustee-to-trustee transfers are NOT rollovers (there is no limit on the number of trustee-to-trustee transfers, and they are not reported as distributions or contributions).

#### Tax on Excess Contributions

IRC section 4973 imposes a 6% excise tax on excess HSA contributions remaining in the account at the end of the tax year. See Form 5329 and its instructions.

### Specific Instructions

#### Name and Social Security Number (SSN)

Enter your name(s) as shown on your tax return. Enter the SSN of the HSA account beneficiary.

If you are married filing jointly and both you and your spouse each have their own HSAs, complete a separate Form 8889 for each spouse.
