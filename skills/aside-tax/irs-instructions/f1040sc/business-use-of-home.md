#### Line 30

**Business use of your home.** If you use part of your home for business, you may be able to deduct the expenses for the business use of your home. The deduction is limited to your gross income from the business use of your home minus the business expenses (other than those for the use of your home). Generally, only two methods are available to figure the deduction: the simplified method or actual expense method using Form 8829.

**Simplified method.** The simplified method allows you to deduct a standard amount for each square foot of your home used for business (up to 300 square feet). For 2025, the rate is $5 per square foot. To use the simplified method, multiply the business-use square footage by $5. For example, if you use 100 square feet of your home for business, your deduction is $500 (100 x $5).

**Electing to use the simplified method.** You can elect to use the simplified method without IRS approval. No election statement is required. Simply use the Simplified Method Worksheet (see below) to figure your deduction and enter the amount on line 30.

**Instructions for the Simplified Method Worksheet**

**Step 1.** Calculate the allowable square footage. The maximum square footage is 300 square feet.

**Step 2.** Multiply the square footage from Step 1 by $5. This is your tentative allowable deduction.

**Step 3.** Calculate your gross income from the business use of your home (the income generated from using part of your home for business purposes).

**Step 4.** Calculate the allowable business expenses (other than expenses for the business use of your home, such as salaries, supplies, etc.).

**Step 5.** Subtract Step 4 from Step 3. If the result is zero or less, your deduction is zero, and you cannot deduct any expenses for business use of your home.

**Step 6.** Compare Step 2 and Step 5. Enter the smaller amount on line 30 of Schedule C.

**Business use of more than one home.** If you use more than one home in connection with your business, you must allocate the business use square footage among all of the homes. The total business use square footage cannot exceed 300 square feet across all homes.

**Other requirements must still be met.** You must meet additional requirements to deduct any business use of home expenses, whether using the simplified method or the actual expense method. You must:

* Use the space exclusively and regularly for business purposes.
* Use the entire home for both business and personal purposes. (Special rules apply if you are a daycare provider.)

**Gross income limitation.** Your deduction for the business use of your home cannot exceed your gross income from the business use of your home minus business expenses (other than those for the use of your home). Any excess deduction is carried forward to the next year.

**Carryover of actual expenses from Form 8829.** If you used the actual expense method on Form 8829 in prior years but want to switch to the simplified method for 2025, any unused actual expenses from prior years (other than depreciation) can be carried forward indefinitely. However, once you elect to use the simplified method, you cannot go back to the actual expense method without IRS approval.

**Carryover of actual expenses for Form 1040-SS filers.** Form 1040-SS filers can carry over any actual expenses from prior years and continue to claim them on Schedule C for the tax year. These amounts will be shown separately from the simplified method deduction or the Form 8829 amounts.

**Depreciation of home.** If you claim actual expenses using Form 8829, you may have to claim depreciation on your home. Depreciation recapture may apply when you sell your home. See Form 8829 and Pub. 587 for details.

**Figuring your allowable expenses for business use of the home.** You can deduct the following expenses for your business use of home using either method.

* Mortgage interest (the business percentage).
* Real estate taxes (the business percentage).
* Utilities (the business percentage).
* Insurance (the business percentage).
* Repairs and maintenance (the business percentage).
* Depreciation (actual method only).

**Using Form 8829.** If you want to deduct actual expenses rather than using the simplified method, complete Form 8829. The actual expense method allows you to deduct larger deductions when you have significant business-related home expenses.

**Using the simplified method.** If you choose the simplified method, you do not deduct individual home expenses. Instead, use the Simplified Method Worksheet above.

**Shared use (for simplified method only).** If more than one person uses the same home office, you can allocate the deductible expenses based on the percent of time each person uses the space. Each person using the space must meet the exclusive use requirement.

**Example.** You use 100 square feet of your home exclusively for business. Your spouse uses the same 100-square-foot space exclusively for their business. Each person can deduct 50 square feet x $5 = $250. Combined deduction is $500.

**Part-year use or area changes (for simplified method only).** If you use an area for business during only part of the year, or if your business-use area changes during the year, you can adjust your deduction accordingly.

**Example 1.** You used 200 square feet for business from January through June (6 months) and 150 square feet from July through December (6 months). Your deduction is: (200 x $5 x 6/12) + (150 x $5 x 6/12) = $500 + $375 = $875.

**Example 2.** You used 250 square feet for the entire year. Your deduction is: 250 x $5 x 12/12 = $1,250.

**Example 3.** You started using 300 square feet for business on September 1 and continued through December 31 (4 months). Your deduction is: 300 x $5 x 4/12 = $500.

**Reporting your expenses for business use of the home.**

**If you used the simplified method.** Enter your deduction from the Simplified Method Worksheet on line 30 of Schedule C.

**Deduction figured on multiple forms.** If your deduction is figured using information from multiple forms (for example, you used the simplified method for part of the year and Form 8829 for another part), combine the amounts and enter the total on line 30.
