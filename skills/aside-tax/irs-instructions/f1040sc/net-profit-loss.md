#### Line 31

**Figuring your net profit or loss.** Add lines 1 through 30 to calculate your total income. Then subtract line 2 (sales returns and allowances) from line 1 to get your net income from operations. Then subtract all deductible expenses (lines 8 through 27b and any amounts from line 48) to calculate your net profit or loss for your business.

If the result is a profit, it is included in calculating your adjusted gross income and is subject to self-employment tax unless an exception applies.

If the result is a loss, you may be able to carry it forward to offset other income in future years, subject to loss limitation rules.

**Reporting your net profit or loss.**

**Individuals.** If line 31 shows a profit, enter that amount on Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 3. If you have a loss, include it in calculating your total loss. You may have limitations on how much of the loss you can deduct. See Form 461 and Pub. 925.

**Nonresident aliens.** Nonresident aliens report net profit or loss differently. See the Instructions for Form 1040-NR for more information.

**Trusts and estates.** Trusts and estates report business income and deductions on Form 1041.

**Statutory employees.** Statutory employees report their net income from Schedule C on Form 1040 or 1040-SR, line 1.

**Notary public.** If your only business is notarizing documents, you should report your income and deductions on Schedule C.

**Community income.** If you and your spouse are using the community property election and reporting business income on one spouse's Schedule C, that spouse reports the total net income from the business.

**Earned income credit (EIC).** Your net profit from Schedule C may qualify for the earned income credit. See the Instructions for Schedule 1 (Form 1040) or IRS.gov for eligibility information.

#### Line 32

**At-risk rules.** Generally, if you have an investment that is not fully at risk, you may not be able to deduct all your losses from the activity. The at-risk rules limit the amount of loss you can deduct to the amount you have invested in the activity that is genuinely at risk.

**Figuring your loss.** If line 31 shows a loss, you may have to use Form 6198 to figure your allowable loss under the at-risk rules.

**All investment is at risk.** If you are fully at risk for all of your investment in the business, you do not have to file Form 6198. Enter your loss from line 31 on line 32.

**Some investment is not at risk.** If some of your investment is not at risk (for example, if you borrowed money to invest in your business without personal liability for repayment), use Form 6198 to figure your deductible loss. Report the deductible loss on line 32.

**At-risk loss deduction.** If your loss is limited by the at-risk rules, you cannot deduct the excess loss in 2025. The excess loss is treated as a deduction from the same activity in the following year.

**More information.** See Form 6198 and section 465 for more details on at-risk rules.
