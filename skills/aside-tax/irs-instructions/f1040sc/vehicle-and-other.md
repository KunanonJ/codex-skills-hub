### Part IV. Information on Your Vehicle

#### Line 44b

If you are claiming the standard mileage rate or using the simplified method for business use of your home, you should complete this part. If you used more than one vehicle, provide the information for each vehicle on attached sheets.

#### Line 47

If you leased a vehicle and the lease period includes a period after 1997, you must reduce your lease payments by any lease inclusion amount. The amount is shown in IRS Publication 946.

### Part V. Other Expenses (Line 48)

The following list includes some common deductible business expenses. If you have an expense not listed, you can report it here.

**Amortization.** Amortization is similar to depreciation. It allows you to recover the cost of certain intangible business assets (such as patents, copyrights, and goodwill) or startup costs over a specified period. You must file Form 4562 to claim amortization.

**At-risk loss deduction.** If you have a loss that exceeds the amount you are at risk for, you cannot deduct the excess loss in the current year. See Form 6198.

**Bad debts.** If you use the accrual method of accounting and had customers or clients who did not pay amounts owed to you, you may be able to deduct the unpaid amounts as bad debts. You cannot deduct business bad debts if you use the cash method of accounting.

**Business startup costs.** You can deduct business startup costs over a period of 15 years (180 months). The amount you can deduct in the first year includes up to $5,000 of startup costs (reduced by any excess over $50,000). The remaining startup costs are amortized over 180 months starting with the month your business begins. You must file Form 4562 to claim the deduction.

**Deduction for removing barriers to individuals with disabilities and the elderly.** You can deduct up to $15,000 per year for expenses to remove barriers to access for individuals with disabilities and elderly individuals from your business property. This includes expenditures for parking, entrances, aisles, restrooms, elevators, escalators, and similar facilities. You can treat these expenses as a current deduction or capitalize them. See section 190 for more information.

**Nontaxable Medicaid waiver payments.** See Line 1 for information on nontaxable Medicaid waiver payments that you should report here in Part V.

**De minimis safe harbor for tangible property.** If you have acquired tangible property with a cost of less than a certain threshold amount, you may be able to deduct the cost immediately rather than depreciating it over time. The de minimis safe harbor allows you to deduct the cost of property that costs less than $2,500 per item (or $5,000 if you have an accounting method for tracking these items). You must have written accounting procedures in place to use this safe harbor.

**Film, television, live theatrical, and sound recording production expenses.** Subject to certain requirements, you can elect to deduct the cost of producing a qualified film, television, live theatrical, or sound recording production in the year incurred. The election must be made by the time you file your tax return (including extensions). See section 181 for more information.

**Forestation and reforestation costs.** You can deduct forestation and reforestation expenses relating to your business.

**Technology and software tools.** You can deduct the cost of technology and software tools used in your business, such as subscriptions to software, apps, or online platforms. However, costs that add substantial value to your business should be capitalized and depreciated over time.
