### Gifts to Charity

Deduct contributions to religious, charitable, educational, scientific, or literary organizations. Also deductible: organizations preventing cruelty to children/animals. Certain whaling captains may deduct 2025 Native Alaskan subsistence bowhead whale hunting expenses (see Publication 526).

Verify organization charitable status by:

- Asking the organization.
- Checking IRS.gov/TEOS using Publication 78 data.

#### Examples of Qualified Charitable Organizations

- Churches, mosques, synagogues, temples, religious organizations.
- Scouts BSA, Boys and Girls Clubs of America, CARE, Girl Scouts, Goodwill Industries, Red Cross, Salvation Army, United Way.
- Fraternal orders (gifts for stated purposes).
- Veterans' and certain cultural groups.
- Nonprofit hospitals and medical research organizations.
- Most nonprofit educational organizations (colleges only if not tuition/fee substitutes).
- Federal, state, local governments (public purpose gifts only).

See Publication 526 for additional examples.

#### Amounts You Can Deduct

Contributions: cash, property, or volunteer work out-of-pocket expenses. Volunteer driving to/from work: actual gas/oil cost or 14 cents per mile; add parking/tolls. Exclude reimbursed amounts.

#### Gifts From Which You Benefit

If receiving benefit (food, entertainment, merchandise), generally deduct only amount exceeding benefit value. Exception: membership benefits under $75 annually or token-value items (see Publication 526).

**Example.** Paid $70 to attend fundraising dinner valued at $40. Deduct $30.

#### Gifts of $250 or More

Need contemporaneous written acknowledgment from charity showing:

1. Money amount and property description (not value).
2. Whether charity provided goods/services in return. If yes, include description and value estimate. If only intangible religious benefits (ceremony admission), state this without description/valuation requirement.

Separate donations don't combine (e.g., $25 weekly church donations = separate gifts). Payroll deductions treated as separate per paycheck donations (see Publication 526 for payroll deduction rules).

Written acknowledgment must arrive by return filing date or due date, whichever earlier. Don't attach acknowledgment; keep for records.

#### Limit on Deductible Amount

See Publication 526 if any apply:

1. Cash/ordinary income property contributions greater than 30% of Form 1040/1040-SR line 11b.
2. Capital gain property gifts greater than 20% of line 11b.
3. Appreciated/use property gifts.

#### Amounts You Can't Deduct

- Charitable contributions requiring state/local tax credit reduction (certain exceptions apply; see Publication 526).
- College/university athletic event ticket purchase rights payments.
- Volunteer travel expenses (meals, lodging) away from home (unless no significant recreation/vacation element).
- Political contributions.
- Country club, lodge, fraternal order, similar group dues/fees/bills.
- Raffle, bingo, lottery ticket costs (possible line 16 deduction; see later).
- Your time/services value.
- Blood bank donations value.
- Future tangible personal property interest transfers (generally no deduction until complete transfer).
- Individual/profit-operated group gifts.
- Foreign organization gifts (exceptions: certain U.S. organizations transferring to foreign charities, Canadian/Israeli/Mexican charities; see Publication 526).
- Organizations engaged in certain political activities with direct trade/business financial interest (section 170(f)(9)).
- Law-change lobbying group gifts.
- Civic leagues, social/sports clubs, labor unions, chambers of commerce gifts.
- Contribution-connected benefit values (Publication 526 exceptions).
- Tuition costs (education credits possible; see Form 8863).

#### Line 11 - Gifts by Cash or Check

Enter total cash/check gifts (including out-of-pocket expenses) unless deduction limits apply. See Publication 526 for limit details. May have carryover to next year.

##### Deduction for Gifts by Cash or Check Limited

If deduction is limited, see Publication 526 for deductible amount. Enter only deductible cash/check gift value on line 11.

##### Recordkeeping

For any cash contribution regardless of amount, maintain bank record (canceled check, credit card statement) or written charity record showing name, date, and amount. Payroll deduction contributions need specific records (see Publication 526). Don't attach records; keep with tax records.

For $250 or more contributions, need contemporaneous written acknowledgment (see Gifts of $250 or more earlier). Retain contribution timing record if acknowledgment doesn't include it.

#### Line 12 - Gifts Other Than Cash or Check

Enter property contribution value unless deduction limits apply. See Publication 526. May have carryover to next year.

##### Deduction for Gifts Other Than Cash or Check Limited

If deduction is limited, see Publication 526. Enter only deductible property gift value on line 12.

##### Valuing Contributions of Used Items

For used items, deduct fair market value at gift time. Fair market value = willing buyer/seller price (neither forced, both aware of sale conditions). See Publication 561 for details.

##### Deduction More Than $500

Complete and attach Form 8283. "Deduction amount" = pre-income-limit deduction.

##### Contribution of Motor Vehicle, Boat, or Airplane

Over $500 deduction requires charity statement attachment to paper return. Form 1098-C may provide information. Over $5,000 deduction may require property appraisals (see Form 8283).

##### Contributions of Clothing and Household Items

Deductible only in good used condition or better. Exception: single items deducted over $500 with qualified appraisal and Form 8283 attached to return.

##### Recordkeeping

Keep organization receipt/statement or reliable record showing organization name, address, gift date/location, and property description. For each property gift, keep reliable records including:

- Property value determination method at gift time (keep signed appraisal copy if appraised).
- Property cost/basis if must reduce for ordinary income/capital gain amounts.
- Deduction calculation method if choosing capital gain property deduction reduction.
- Gift conditions.

For $250 or more property gifts, need contemporaneous written acknowledgment (see Gifts of $250 or more). Form 8283 doesn't satisfy acknowledgment requirement; acknowledgment isn't substitute for other needed records.

**Caution.** For over $500 property gift deductions, entire property interest gifts, or qualified conservation contributions, records need additional information (see Publication 526).

#### Line 13 - Carryover From Prior Year

May have prior contributions exceeding deductible limits. Generally 5-year period available for limited contributions. Same limits apply to carryover as original year. However, 2021 carryovers in 2025 subject to 60% limit. After applying limits, enter allowable carryover deduction. See Publication 526.
