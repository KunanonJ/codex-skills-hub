### Interest You Paid

Deductible interest rules vary by loan usage (business, personal, investment). See Form 8990 instructions for business interest. See Publication 550 for investment interest. Personal interest isn't deductible. Qualified home mortgage interest (Schedule A) and certain student loan interest (Schedule 1, line 21) are deductible per Publications 936 and 970.

If loan proceeds serve multiple purposes, allocate interest by tracing disbursements to specific uses.

Generally, if 2025 interest applies to periods after 2025, deduct only 2025 amounts.

Use Schedule A for qualified home mortgage interest and investment interest.

#### Line 8 - Home Mortgage Interest

Home mortgage = any loan secured by main or second home, regardless of labeling. Includes first/second mortgages, home equity loans, refinanced mortgages.

Home = house, condominium, cooperative, mobile home, boat, similar property providing basic living accommodations (sleeping space, toilet, cooking facilities).

Surviving spouses may deduct post-death mortgage interest. For decedent-paid interest, see Forms 1041, 706, and instructions.

Check line 8 box if you had one or more 2025 home mortgages with outstanding balance not entirely used to buy, build, or substantially improve the home.

**Tip.** If using home mortgage proceeds for business/investment purposes, related interest may be deductible elsewhere on your return.

##### Limits on Home Mortgage Interest

Deduction subject to several limits. Consult Publication 936 if limits apply.

##### Limit for Loan Proceeds Not Used to Buy, Build, or Substantially Improve Home

Deduct home mortgage interest only to extent loan proceeds used to buy, build, or substantially improve the home ("qualifying debt"). Check line 8 box if 2025 home mortgages with outstanding balance didn't use all proceeds for qualifying purposes. Exception: loans before October 13, 1987 (proceeds treated as home-improving). See Publication 936 for details.

##### Limit on Loans Taken Out On or Before December 15, 2017

For pre-December 15, 2017 qualifying debt, deduct interest on up to $1,000,000 ($500,000 if married filing separately). Exception: pre-October 13, 1987 loans (see Publication 936).

##### Limit on Loans Taken Out After December 15, 2017

For post-December 15, 2017 qualifying debt, deduct interest on up to $750,000 ($375,000 if married filing separately). If having pre-December 15, 2017 qualifying debt, reduce $750,000 limit by that pre-2017 debt amount. Certain April 1, 2018-exception loans may receive pre-December 15, 2017 treatment (see Publication 936).

##### Limit When Loans Exceed Fair Market Value of Home

If total mortgages exceed home fair market value, see Publication 936.

#### Line 8a - Home Mortgage Interest Reported on Form 1098

Enter mortgage interest and points reported on Form 1098 unless home mortgage interest limits apply.

##### Home Mortgage Interest Limited

If deduction is limited, see Publication 936 for deductible Form 1098 mortgage interest/points. Enter only deductible amounts on line 8a.

##### Refund of Overpaid Interest

If Form 1098 shows overpaid interest refund, don't reduce deduction. Instead, see Schedule 1 instructions, line 8z.

##### More Than One Borrower

If you and other(s) (besides spouse if joint return) were liable and paid mortgage interest, deduct only your share.

##### Shared Interest Reported on Your Form 1098

If shared interest was reported on received Form 1098, deduct only your share on line 8a. Inform other borrowers of their shares.

##### Shared Interest Reported on Someone Else's Form 1098

If shared interest was reported on other person's Form 1098, report your share on line 8b.

##### Form 1098 Doesn't Show All Interest Paid

If paid more interest than Form 1098 shows, include larger deductible amount on line 8a with explanation. If filing paper return, attach statement to return and print "See attached" right of line 8a.

**Caution.** If claiming mortgage interest credit (Form 8396, qualified mortgage credit certificate), subtract line 3 from total deductible interest paid, enter result on line 8a.

#### Line 8b - Home Mortgage Interest Not Reported on Form 1098

Report deductible mortgage interest paid to recipients without providing Form 1098.

##### Seller Financed Mortgage

If paying home mortgage interest to seller and no Form 1098 provided, write recipient's name, identifying number (SSN for individuals, EIN otherwise), and address on dotted lines next to line 8b. Let recipient know your SSN.

**Caution.** Not showing required recipient information or letting recipient know your SSN may result in $50 penalty.

##### Interest Reported on Someone Else's Form 1098

If you and others (besides spouse if jointly filing) were liable/paid interest with interest reported on another's Form 1098, identify that person/persons' names and addresses. If filing paper return, attach statement and print "See attached" right of line 8b.

#### Line 8c - Points Not Reported on Form 1098

Points appear on settlement statements. Points paid to borrow money are generally deductible over loan life. See Publication 936.

##### Refinancing

Generally, deduct refinance points over new loan life, even for main home mortgages. If part improves main home, partial deduction may be allowable that year (see Publication 936).

**Tip.** If paying off mortgage early, deduct remaining points that year. Exception if refinancing with same lender (see Publication 936 Mortgage ending early).

#### Line 8d

Reserved for future use.

#### Line 9 - Investment Interest

Investment interest = interest on borrowed money for investment property, excluding passive activities or tax-exempt security amounts.

Complete and attach Form 4952.

##### Exception

Form 4952 unnecessary if all three apply:

1. Investment interest expense is less than investment income from interest/ordinary dividends minus qualified dividends.
2. No other deductible investment expenses.
3. No disallowed 2024 investment interest.

**Caution.** Alaska Permanent Fund dividends (including Form 8814-reported) aren't investment income.

See Publication 550 for details.
