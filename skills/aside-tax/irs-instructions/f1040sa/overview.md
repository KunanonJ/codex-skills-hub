## Instructions for Schedule A (Form 1040) Itemized Deductions

### Future Developments

For the latest information about developments related to Schedule A and its instructions, such as legislation enacted after they were published, go to IRS.gov/ScheduleA.

### What's New

#### State and Local Tax (SALT) Deduction Limit Increased

The overall limit on state and local income, sales, and property tax deductions increased to $40,000 ($20,000 if married filing separately). The limit reduces if modified adjusted gross income exceeds $500,000 ($250,000 if married filing separately) but won't decrease below $10,000 ($5,000 if married filing separately).

#### New Schedule 1-A (Form 1040)

Recent legislation introduced four new deductions beginning 2025: no tax on tips, no tax on overtime, no tax on car loan interest, and an enhanced deduction for seniors. These are claimed on Schedule 1-A, not Schedule A.

### General Instructions

Schedule A is used to calculate itemized deductions. Generally, your federal income tax is lower using whichever is larger: itemized deductions or standard deduction.

Itemizable expenses include portions of medical and dental expenses, certain taxes, interest, contributions, and casualty/theft losses.

For joint expenses on separate 2025 returns, consult Publication 504.

**Important.** Don't include items deducted elsewhere on Form 1040, Form 1040-SR, Schedule 1-A, or Schedules C, E, or F.
