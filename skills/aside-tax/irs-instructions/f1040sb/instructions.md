## Instructions for Schedule B (Form 1040) Interest and Ordinary Dividends

### General Instructions

#### Purpose of Schedule B

You must complete Schedule B if any of the following apply:

- You had over $1,500 of taxable interest or ordinary dividends.
- You received interest from a seller-financed mortgage and the buyer used the property as a personal residence.
- You have accrued interest from a bond.
- You are reporting original issue discount (OID) in an amount less than the amount shown on Form 1099-OID.
- You are reducing your interest income on a bond by the amount of amortizable bond premium.
- You are claiming the exclusion of interest from series EE or I U.S. savings bonds issued after 1989.
- You received interest or ordinary dividends as a nominee.
- You had a financial interest in, or signature authority over, a financial account in a foreign country, or you received a distribution from, or were a grantor of, or transferor to, a foreign trust.

### Specific Instructions

### Part I -- Interest

#### Line 1

Report on line 1 all of your taxable interest. Taxable interest generally should be shown on your Forms 1099-INT and 1099-OID. Include interest income from series EE, H, HH, and I U.S. savings bonds. Also include any accrued market discount that is taxable and any gain on a contingent payment debt instrument that is taxable as interest income.

Don't report on line 1 any tax-exempt interest. Instead, report tax-exempt interest on Form 1040 or 1040-SR, line 2a.

**Nominees.** If you received a Form 1099-INT or Form 1099-OID that includes interest you received as a nominee (that is, in your name, but the interest actually belongs to someone else), report the total on line 1. Do this even if you later distributed some or all of this income to others. Under the last entry on line 1, put a subtotal of all interest listed on line 1. Below this subtotal, enter "Nominee Distribution" and show the total interest you received as a nominee. Subtract this amount from the subtotal and enter the result on line 4.

You must give the actual owner of the interest a Form 1099-INT (unless the owner is your spouse). You must also file Form(s) 1099-INT and a Form 1096 with the IRS. For more details, including penalties, see the General Instructions for Certain Information Returns.

**Exceptions to reporting as nominee.** You do not have to file a Form 1099-INT for interest received as a nominee if any of the following conditions apply:

- The actual owner of the interest is your spouse.
- You are a participant in a joint account with a non-spouse and there was no nominee arrangement.

**Accrued interest.** If you bought a bond between interest payment dates, part of the purchase price represents accrued interest from the last interest payment date to the purchase date. When you receive your first interest payment, it will include the accrued interest you paid at the time of purchase. If you received a Form 1099-INT for the interest, follow the rules for nominees above to determine the amounts to report on line 1. Under the last entry on line 1, put a subtotal of all interest listed on line 1. Below this subtotal, enter "Accrued Interest" and show the accrued interest you paid. Subtract this amount from the subtotal and enter the result on line 4.

**Original issue discount (OID) adjustment.** If you are reporting OID on a taxable bond in an amount less than the OID shown on Form 1099-OID, follow the rules for nominees above to determine the amounts to report on line 1. Under the last entry on line 1, put a subtotal of all interest listed on line 1. Below this subtotal, enter "OID Adjustment" and show the amount by which you are reducing the OID. Subtract this amount from the subtotal and enter the result on line 4. For more information on OID, see Publication 550.

**Amortizable bond premium (ABP) adjustment.** If you are reducing your interest income on a taxable bond by the amount of amortizable bond premium, follow the rules for nominees above to determine the amounts to report on line 1. Under the last entry on line 1, put a subtotal of all interest listed on line 1. Below this subtotal, enter "ABP Adjustment" and show the amount of the adjustment. Subtract this amount from the subtotal and enter the result on line 4. For more information on ABP, see Publication 550.

**Tax-exempt interest.** Don't include tax-exempt interest on line 1. Tax-exempt interest includes interest from municipal bonds and any exempt-interest dividends from a mutual fund or other regulated investment company. Interest from certain private activity bonds may be taxable if subject to the alternative minimum tax. For additional information, see Form 6251 instructions.

If you received tax-exempt interest from series EE or I U.S. savings bonds issued after 1989 that you used for qualified higher education expenses, report the interest on Form 1040 or 1040-SR, line 2a.

#### Line 3 - Excludible Interest on Series EE and I U.S. Savings Bonds Issued After 1989

If you cashed series EE or I U.S. savings bonds in 2025 that were issued after 1989 and you paid qualified higher education expenses for yourself, your spouse, or your dependents during 2025, you may be able to exclude part or all of the interest on those bonds. See Form 8815 for details. Enter the excludible amount from Form 8815 on line 3.

### Part II -- Ordinary Dividends

#### Line 5

Report on line 5 all of your ordinary dividends. This includes any ordinary dividends shown in box 1a of all the Forms 1099-DIV you received.

**Nominees.** If you received a Form 1099-DIV that includes ordinary dividends you received as a nominee (that is, in your name, but the ordinary dividends actually belong to someone else), report the total on line 5. Do this even if you later distributed some or all of this income to others. Under the last entry on line 5, put a subtotal of all ordinary dividends listed on line 5. Below this subtotal, enter "Nominee Distribution" and show the total ordinary dividends you received as a nominee. Subtract this amount from the subtotal and enter the result on line 6.

You must give the actual owner of the dividends a Form 1099-DIV (unless the owner is your spouse). You must also file Form(s) 1099-DIV and a Form 1096 with the IRS. For more details, including penalties, see the General Instructions for Certain Information Returns.

### Part III -- Foreign Accounts and Trusts

You must complete Part III if:

- You had a financial interest in or signature authority over a financial account in a foreign country at any time during 2025, or
- You received a distribution from, or were a grantor of, or transferor to, a foreign trust.

#### Line 7a -- Question 1

Check "Yes" if, at any time during 2025, you had a financial interest in or signature authority (or other authority that is comparable to signature authority) over a financial account in a foreign country (such as a bank account, securities account, or other financial account).

##### Financial Account

A financial account includes, but is not limited to:

- Securities, brokerage, savings, demand, checking, deposit, time deposit, or other account maintained with a financial institution (or other person performing the services of a financial institution).
- A commodity futures or options account.
- An insurance policy with a cash value (such as a whole life insurance policy).
- An annuity policy with a cash value.
- Shares in a mutual fund or similar pooled fund (that is, a fund that is available to the general public with a regular net asset value determination and regular redemptions).

##### Foreign Country

A foreign country includes all geographical areas outside the United States, including:

- U.S. bank branches that are physically located in a foreign country are foreign financial accounts.
- Foreign bank branches that are physically located in the United States are not foreign financial accounts.

##### Signature Authority

Signature authority is the authority of an individual (alone or in conjunction with another individual) to control the disposition of assets held in a foreign financial account by direct communication (whether in writing or otherwise) to the bank or other financial institution that maintains the financial account.

#### Line 7a -- Question 2

Check "Yes" if you are required to file FinCEN Form 114 (FBAR), Report of Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts. Generally, you must file FinCEN Form 114 if:

- You had a financial interest in, or signature authority over, at least one financial account located outside the United States, and
- The aggregate value of all foreign financial accounts exceeded $10,000 at any time during the calendar year.

FinCEN Form 114 must be filed electronically through the BSA E-Filing System. The due date for filing is April 15, 2026, with an automatic extension to October 15, 2026. You don't need to request this extension.

##### Penalties

Civil and criminal penalties may apply for failing to file FinCEN Form 114 when required:

- Civil penalties up to $10,000 for non-willful violations.
- For willful violations, the penalty is the greater of $100,000 or 50% of the balance of the account at the time of the violation.
- Criminal penalties may also apply, including fines and imprisonment.

##### Exceptions to FinCEN Form 114 Filing

Certain persons are exempt from filing FinCEN Form 114. See the FinCEN Form 114 instructions at fincen.gov for details about exemptions.

#### Line 7b

If you checked "Yes" to any question on line 7a, enter the name of each foreign country in which you had a financial account.

#### Line 8 -- Foreign Trusts

Check "Yes" if you received a distribution from, or were a grantor of, or a transferor to, a foreign trust. If "Yes," you may need to file Form 3520.

##### Distributions From Foreign Trusts

A distribution from a foreign trust includes the receipt of any cash or other property (including a loan of cash or securities) and the use of any trust property. A "foreign trust" is any trust that is not a U.S. trust (a trust is a U.S. trust only if a court within the United States is able to exercise primary supervision over the administration of the trust and one or more U.S. persons have authority to control all substantial decisions of the trust).

If you received a distribution from a foreign trust, you may need to file Form 3520, Annual Return To Report Transactions With Foreign Trusts and Receipt of Certain Foreign Gifts, with the IRS by the due date (including extensions) of your income tax return. See the Instructions for Form 3520 for more information.

##### Grantors of and Transferors to Foreign Trusts

If you were treated as the owner of any part of a foreign trust under the grantor trust rules, you may need to ensure that the foreign trust files Form 3520-A, Annual Information Return of Foreign Trust With a U.S. Owner, by the 15th day of the 3rd month after the end of the trust's tax year (March 16, 2026, for a trust with a calendar year).

If you transferred property (including cash) to a foreign trust, you may need to file Form 3520 with the IRS by the due date (including extensions) of your income tax return. See the Instructions for Form 3520 for details.

##### Form 8938

You may also need to file Form 8938, Statement of Specified Foreign Financial Assets, if you had certain foreign financial assets during 2025 and the total value of those assets exceeded certain thresholds. This requirement applies even if the accounts are reported on FinCEN Form 114. Failure to file Form 8938 may result in penalties and may extend the statute of limitations on your return.

See the Instructions for Form 8938 for the filing thresholds and other information.
