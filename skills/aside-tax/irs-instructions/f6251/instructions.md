## Instructions for Form 6251 - Alternative Minimum Tax—Individuals

### Future Developments

For the latest information about developments related to Form 6251 and its instructions, such as legislation enacted after they were published, go to IRS.gov/Form6251.

### General Instructions

#### Purpose of Form

Use Form 6251 to figure the amount of your alternative minimum tax (AMT). The AMT is a separate tax that is imposed in addition to your regular tax. It applies to taxpayers who have certain types of income that receive favorable treatment, or who qualify for certain deductions, under the tax law. These tax benefits can significantly reduce the regular tax of some taxpayers with higher economic incomes. The AMT sets a limit on the amount these benefits can be used to reduce total tax.

Attach Form 6251 to your Form 1040, 1040-SR, or 1040-NR.

#### Who Must File

You must attach Form 6251 to your return if any of the following apply:

- Form 6251, line 7, is greater than line 10.
- You claim any general business credit, and the tentative minimum tax (from Form 3800, line 6, or, if applicable, line 25) exceeds zero.
- You claim the qualified electric vehicle credit (Form 8834), the personal use part of the alternative fuel vehicle refueling property credit (Form 8911), or the credit for prior year minimum tax (Form 8801).
- The total of lines 2c through 3 is negative and line 7 would be greater than line 10 if you did not take into account lines 2c through 3.

**Tip.** Even if you do not have to file Form 6251, you may want to figure the AMT to see if you owe it. To do this, complete lines 1 through 11 of Form 6251. If line 7 is greater than line 10, you owe AMT.

### 2025 AMT Exemption Amounts and Phase-Outs

| Filing Status | Exemption Amount | Phase-Out Begins | Complete Phase-Out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single or Head of Household | $88,100 | $626,350 | $978,750 |
| Married Filing Jointly or Qualifying Surviving Spouse | $137,000 | $1,252,700 | $1,800,700 |
| Married Filing Separately | $68,500 | $626,350 | $900,350 |

The exemption amount is reduced by 25 cents for each dollar that AMTI exceeds the phase-out threshold.

### 2025 AMT Tax Rates

| AMT Taxable Amount (Line 6) | Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to $239,100 ($119,550 if MFS) | 26% |
| Over $239,100 ($119,550 if MFS) | 28% |

### Specific Instructions

#### Part I — Alternative Minimum Taxable Income

**Line 1a.** Subtract Schedule 1-A (Form 1040), line 37, from Form 1040, 1040-SR, or 1040-NR, line 14. This removes the Schedule 1-A deductions from your total deductions amount for AMT purposes.

**Line 1b.** Subtract line 1a from Form 1040, 1040-SR, or 1040-NR, line 11b. If the result is less than zero, enter it as a negative amount. This is the starting point for computing your AMTI.

**Line 2a — Taxes.** If you are filing Schedule A (Form 1040), enter the taxes from Schedule A, line 7 (but do not include any generation-skipping transfer taxes). If you are not itemizing deductions, enter the amount from Form 1040 or 1040-SR, line 12e (your standard deduction). State and local taxes deducted on Schedule A are not allowed for AMT purposes.

**Line 2b — Tax Refund.** Enter as a negative amount any tax refund from Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 1 or line 8z. This reverses any state/local tax refund included in income, since the underlying deduction is not allowed for AMT.

**Line 2c — Investment Interest Expense.** Refigure your investment interest expense deduction by completing an AMT version of Form 4952, including any interest from private activity bonds as investment income. Enter the difference between the AMT and regular tax amounts from Form 4952, line 8.

**Line 2d — Depletion.** Refigure your depletion deduction using only income and deductions allowed for the AMT. Enter the difference between your regular tax and AMT depletion amounts.

**Line 2e — Net Operating Loss Deduction.** Enter as a positive amount your net operating loss deduction from Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 8a.

**Line 2f — Alternative Tax Net Operating Loss Deduction (ATNOLD).** Enter as a negative amount. The ATNOLD is generally limited to 80% of AMTI (figured without the ATNOLD). Special rules may apply to qualified disaster losses and certain other losses. This replaces the regular tax NOL deduction for AMT purposes.

**Line 2g — Private Activity Bond Interest.** Enter interest from specified private activity bonds that is exempt from regular tax, reduced by any related deductions that would be allowable if the interest were includible in gross income.

**Line 2h — Qualified Small Business Stock (Section 1202).** If you claimed an exclusion for qualified small business stock acquired before September 28, 2010, and held for more than 5 years, multiply the excluded gain by 7% and enter the result.

**Line 2i — Exercise of Incentive Stock Options (ISO).** Enter the excess of the fair market value of the stock (determined without regard to any lapse restriction) over the amount you paid for it (the exercise price). Include the amount when your rights in the stock are first transferable or are no longer subject to a substantial risk of forfeiture. This is one of the most common AMT adjustments.

**Line 2j — Estates and Trusts.** Enter the amount from Schedule K-1 (Form 1041), box 12, code A.

**Line 2k — Disposition of Property.** Refigure your gain or loss from Forms 4684, 4797, 8949, and Schedule D using AMT adjusted basis. The AMT adjusted basis reflects differences in depreciation and other AMT adjustments. Apply the $3,000 capital loss limitation separately for the AMT.

**Line 2l — Depreciation on Assets Placed in Service After 1986.** Enter the difference between your regular tax and AMT depreciation.

You must refigure depreciation for the AMT for:
- Property placed in service after 1998 for which you used the 200% declining balance method
- Section 1250 property placed in service after 1998 not depreciated using the straight-line method
- Tangible property placed in service from 1987 through 1998

You do not need to refigure depreciation for:
- Residential rental property placed in service after 1998
- Nonresidential real property with a recovery period of 27.5 years or more using straight-line
- Property depreciated using 150% declining balance or straight-line methods
- Property for which you elected the alternative depreciation system (ADS)
- Qualified property for which you claimed the special depreciation allowance

For the AMT, use the 150% declining balance method switching to straight-line, over the AMT class life. For section 1250 property, use straight-line over the AMT class life.

**Line 2m — Passive Activities.** Refigure your passive activity gain or loss using AMT adjustments to income and deductions from passive activities, and AMT prior year unallowed losses. You may need to complete an AMT version of Form 8582.

**Line 2n — Loss Limitations.** Refigure your at-risk limitations and partnership or S corporation basis limitations using AMT adjustments. Enter the difference between the regular tax and AMT amounts.

**Line 2o — Circulation Costs.** For AMT, circulation expenditures must be capitalized and amortized over 3 years, rather than deducted in the year incurred.

**Line 2p — Long-Term Contracts.** For AMT, use the percentage-of-completion method for long-term contracts entered into after February 28, 1986. Enter the difference between the AMT and regular tax income.

**Line 2q — Mining Costs.** For AMT, mining exploration and development costs must be capitalized and amortized over 10 years. Enter the difference between the regular tax and AMT amounts.

**Line 2r — Research and Experimental Costs.** For AMT, research and experimental expenditures must be capitalized and amortized over 10 years. Enter the difference between the regular tax and AMT amounts.

**Line 2s — Installment Sales Before January 1, 1987.** For certain pre-1987 installment sales, enter the regular tax income from installment payments as a negative amount.

**Line 2t — Intangible Drilling Costs Preference.** The IDC preference applies when excess intangible drilling costs exceed 65% of net oil and gas income. Independent producers may receive limited benefit from this preference.

**Line 3 — Other Adjustments.** Include adjustments for:
- Pre-1987 depreciation adjustments
- Pollution control facilities amortization differences
- Tax shelter farm activity adjustments
- Charitable contribution property basis differences (AMT vs. regular tax)
- Business interest limitation adjustments (section 163(j))
- Mortgage interest on non-qualified dwelling loans
- Net qualified disaster loss standard deduction amounts

**Line 4 — Alternative Minimum Taxable Income (AMTI).** Combine lines 1b through 3. If married filing separately and line 4 is more than $900,350, you must increase AMTI: add back the lesser of (a) 25% of the excess over $900,350, or (b) $68,500. If line 4 is $1,174,350 or more, add back $68,500.

#### Part II — Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT)

**Line 5 — Exemption.**

If your AMTI (line 4) does not exceed the phase-out threshold for your filing status, enter the full exemption amount:
- $88,100 if Single or Head of Household
- $137,000 if Married Filing Jointly or Qualifying Surviving Spouse
- $68,500 if Married Filing Separately

If your AMTI exceeds the phase-out threshold, complete the Exemption Worksheet:
1. Enter your AMTI from line 4.
2. Enter the phase-out threshold for your filing status ($626,350 for Single/HOH, $1,252,700 for MFJ/QSS, $626,350 for MFS).
3. Subtract step 2 from step 1. If zero or less, enter the full exemption on line 5.
4. Multiply step 3 by 25% (0.25).
5. Subtract step 4 from the exemption amount. If zero or less, enter -0- on line 5.

**Line 6.** Subtract line 5 from line 4. If the result is more than zero, go to line 7. If the result is zero or less, enter -0- here and on lines 7, 9, and 11, and go to line 10.

**Line 7.** Compute the AMT on the amount on line 6:

- **If filing Form 2555:** See the Foreign Earned Income Tax Worksheet in the instructions.
- **If you reported capital gain distributions directly on Form 1040 or 1040-SR, line 7; qualified dividends on Form 1040 or 1040-SR, line 3a; or had a gain on both lines 15 and 16 of Schedule D (as refigured for the AMT, if necessary):** Complete Part III on page 2 and enter the amount from line 40 here.
- **All others:** If line 6 is $239,100 or less ($119,550 or less if married filing separately), multiply line 6 by 26% (0.26). Otherwise, multiply line 6 by 28% (0.28) and subtract $4,782 ($2,391 if married filing separately) from the result.

**Line 8 — Alternative Minimum Tax Foreign Tax Credit.** If you paid or accrued foreign taxes, you may be able to claim an AMT foreign tax credit. Complete a separate AMT version of Form 1116 for each category of income, using AMT-allowed amounts. The AMT foreign tax credit cannot exceed the AMT computed without the credit.

**Line 9 — Tentative Minimum Tax.** Subtract line 8 from line 7.

**Line 10.** Add Form 1040 or 1040-SR, line 16 (minus any tax from Form 4972), and Schedule 2 (Form 1040), line 1z. Subtract from the result Schedule 3 (Form 1040), line 1 and any negative amount reported on Form 8978, line 14 (treated as a positive number). If zero or less, enter -0-. If you used Schedule J to figure your tax on Form 1040 or 1040-SR, line 16, refigure that tax without using Schedule J before completing this line.

**Line 11 — AMT.** Subtract line 10 from line 9. If zero or less, enter -0-. Enter this amount here and on Schedule 2 (Form 1040), line 2. This is your alternative minimum tax.

#### Part III — Tax Computation Using Maximum Capital Gains Rates

Complete Part III only if you are required to do so by line 7 or by the Foreign Earned Income Tax Worksheet.

**Line 12.** Enter the amount from line 6. If you are filing Form 2555, enter the amount from line 3 of the worksheet in the instructions for line 7.

**Line 13.** Enter the amount from line 4 of the Qualified Dividends and Capital Gain Tax Worksheet in the instructions for Form 1040, or the amount from line 13 of the Schedule D Tax Worksheet in the instructions for Schedule D (Form 1040), whichever applies (as refigured for the AMT, if necessary).

**Line 14.** Enter the amount from Schedule D (Form 1040), line 19 (as refigured for the AMT, if necessary). If you are filing Form 2555, see instructions.

**Line 15.** If you did not complete a Schedule D Tax Worksheet for the regular tax or the AMT, enter the amount from line 13. Otherwise, add lines 13 and 14, and enter the smaller of that result or the amount from line 10 of the Schedule D Tax Worksheet (as refigured for the AMT, if necessary).

**Line 16.** Enter the smaller of line 12 or line 15.

**Line 17.** Subtract line 16 from line 12.

**Line 18.** Compute the tax on line 17:
- If line 17 is $239,100 or less ($119,550 or less if MFS), multiply by 26% (0.26).
- Otherwise, multiply by 28% (0.28) and subtract $4,782 ($2,391 if MFS).

**Line 19.** Enter the applicable capital gains rate threshold:
- $96,700 if married filing jointly or qualifying surviving spouse
- $48,350 if single or married filing separately
- $64,750 if head of household

**Line 20.** Enter the amount from line 5 of the Qualified Dividends and Capital Gain Tax Worksheet or line 14 of the Schedule D Tax Worksheet (as figured for the regular tax). If you did not complete either worksheet, enter the amount from Form 1040 or 1040-SR, line 15; if zero or less, enter -0-.

**Line 21.** Subtract line 20 from line 19. If zero or less, enter -0-.

**Line 22.** Enter the smaller of line 12 or line 13.

**Line 23.** Enter the smaller of line 21 or line 22. This amount is taxed at 0%.

**Line 24.** Subtract line 23 from line 22.

**Line 25.** Enter the applicable threshold:
- $533,400 if single
- $300,000 if married filing separately
- $600,050 if married filing jointly or qualifying surviving spouse
- $566,700 if head of household

**Line 26.** Enter the amount from line 21.

**Line 27.** Enter the amount from line 5 of the Qualified Dividends and Capital Gain Tax Worksheet or line 21 of the Schedule D Tax Worksheet (as figured for the regular tax). If you did not complete either worksheet, enter the amount from Form 1040 or 1040-SR, line 15; if zero or less, enter -0-.

**Line 28.** Add line 26 and line 27.

**Line 29.** Subtract line 28 from line 25. If zero or less, enter -0-.

**Line 30.** Enter the smaller of line 24 or line 29.

**Line 31.** Multiply line 30 by 15% (0.15).

**Line 32.** Add lines 23 and 30.

If lines 32 and 12 are the same, skip lines 33 through 37 and go to line 38.

**Line 33.** Subtract line 32 from line 22.

**Line 34.** Multiply line 33 by 20% (0.20).

If line 14 is zero or blank, skip lines 35 through 37 and go to line 38.

**Line 35.** Add lines 17, 32, and 33.

**Line 36.** Subtract line 35 from line 12.

**Line 37.** Multiply line 36 by 25% (0.25).

**Line 38.** Add lines 18, 31, 34, and 37.

**Line 39.** Compute the tax on line 12:
- If line 12 is $239,100 or less ($119,550 or less if MFS), multiply by 26% (0.26).
- Otherwise, multiply by 28% (0.28) and subtract $4,782 ($2,391 if MFS).

**Line 40.** Enter the smaller of line 38 or line 39. Enter this amount on line 7. If you are filing Form 2555, do not enter this amount on line 7; instead, enter it on line 4 of the worksheet in the instructions for line 7.
