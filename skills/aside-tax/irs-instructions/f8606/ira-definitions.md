### General Instructions

#### Purpose of Form

Form 8606 is used to report:

- Nondeductible contributions you made to traditional IRAs;
- Distributions from traditional, SEP, and SIMPLE IRAs, if you have ever made nondeductible contributions to traditional IRAs;
- Conversions from traditional, SEP, or SIMPLE IRAs to Roth IRAs; and
- Distributions from Roth IRAs.

Nondeductible contributions to a traditional IRA do not include employer contributions (including contributions made pursuant to a salary reduction arrangement) made to a SEP IRA under a SEP arrangement or to a SIMPLE IRA under a SIMPLE IRA plan.

For more information on IRAs, see Publication 590-A (Contributions to Individual Retirement Arrangements (IRAs)), Publication 590-B (Distributions from Individual Retirement Arrangements (IRAs)), and Publication 560 (Retirement Plans for Small Business (SEP, SIMPLE, and Qualified Plans)).

#### Who Must File

File Form 8606 if any of the following apply.

1. You made nondeductible contributions to a traditional IRA for 2025.
2. You received distributions from a traditional, SEP, or SIMPLE IRA in 2025 and you have a basis in traditional IRAs. (Use Form 8606 to figure the taxable and nontaxable portions of such distributions.)
3. You transferred all or part of a traditional IRA to your spouse under a divorce or separation agreement and it resulted in a change in basis for either spouse.
4. You converted part or all of your traditional, SEP, or SIMPLE IRAs to Roth IRAs in 2025.
5. You received distributions from a Roth IRA in 2025 (other than a rollover, recharacterization, or return of certain contributions; see Line 19 instructions under Part III).
6. You received distributions from an inherited traditional IRA that had basis, or from an inherited Roth IRA that was not a qualified distribution.

#### When and Where To File

File Form 8606 with your 2025 Form 1040, 1040-SR, or 1040-NR by the due date, including extensions, of your return.

If you are not required to file an income tax return but are required to file Form 8606, sign Form 8606 and send it to the IRS at the same time and place you would otherwise file Form 1040, 1040-SR, or 1040-NR. Be sure to include your address on page 1 and your signature and the date on page 2.

#### Definitions

##### IRAs Generally

An IRA is an individual retirement account or an individual retirement annuity. Individual retirement accounts are trusts or custodial accounts established in the United States for the exclusive benefit of account owners or their beneficiaries. Individual retirement annuities are annuity contracts or endowment contracts purchased from a life insurance company where the owner is entitled to receive all benefits.

IRAs include traditional IRAs, traditional SEP IRAs, traditional SIMPLE IRAs, Roth IRAs, Roth SEP IRAs, and Roth SIMPLE IRAs. Individuals may make contributions to traditional IRAs, traditional SEP IRAs, Roth IRAs, or Roth SEP IRAs, while only employers contribute to SIMPLE IRAs.

**IRA references in these instructions.** Unless otherwise stated:

- Traditional IRAs generally include traditional SEP IRAs and traditional SIMPLE IRAs.
- Roth IRAs generally include Roth SEP IRAs and Roth SIMPLE IRAs.
- Contributions to IRAs will not include employer contributions (including contributions made pursuant to a salary reduction arrangement) to SEP IRAs or SIMPLE IRAs.

##### Deemed IRAs

A qualified employer plan can maintain a separate account or annuity (a deemed IRA) under the plan to receive voluntary employee contributions. If the separate account or annuity otherwise meets the requirements of a traditional IRA or a Roth IRA, it is subject to the rules for traditional IRAs or Roth IRAs, as applicable.

##### Traditional IRAs

An IRA owner may make deductible or nondeductible contributions to traditional IRAs. You can open and make contributions to a traditional IRA if you (or, if you file a joint return, your spouse) received taxable compensation during the year.

**Contributions.** An overall contribution limit applies to all traditional IRA contributions that aren't employer contributions to a SEP IRA or a SIMPLE IRA. See Overall Contribution Limit, later. Contributions may be fully deductible, partially deductible, or completely nondeductible, depending on your filing status, whether you or your spouse is covered by an employer retirement plan, and your modified adjusted gross income.

**Basis.** Your basis in traditional IRAs is the total of all your nondeductible contributions and your nontaxable amounts included in rollovers made to these IRAs, minus the total of all your nontaxable distributions, adjusted if necessary. Track basis carefully to determine the nontaxable portion of future distributions.

##### Roth IRAs

Roth IRAs are similar to traditional IRAs but offer distinct features: contributions are never deductible, no minimum distributions are required during the owner's lifetime, and qualified distributions are not includible in income.

**Qualified distribution.** Generally, a qualified distribution from a Roth IRA is one that meets both of the following requirements.

1. It is made after the 5-year period beginning with the first taxable year for which a contribution was made to a Roth IRA set up for your benefit.
2. The distribution is made:
   - On or after the date you reach age 59-1/2,
   - After your death,
   - Because you are disabled, or
   - For qualified first-time homebuyer expenses (up to a $10,000 lifetime limit).

**Contributions.** You can contribute to a Roth IRA for 2025 only if your modified AGI for Roth IRA purposes is less than:

- $246,000 if married filing jointly or qualifying surviving spouse,
- $165,000 if single, head of household, or married filing separately and you didn't live with your spouse at any time in 2025, or
- $10,000 if married filing separately and you lived with your spouse at any time in 2025.

Use the Maximum Roth IRA Contribution Worksheet (later in these instructions) to determine the maximum amount you can contribute to a Roth IRA for 2025.

##### SEP IRAs: Traditional and Roth

A simplified employee pension (SEP) arrangement is an employer-sponsored plan under which an employer can make contributions to a traditional SEP IRA or a Roth SEP IRA for its employees. Personal contributions to a traditional SEP IRA are treated as traditional IRA contributions and may be deductible or nondeductible. Personal contributions to a Roth SEP IRA are treated as Roth IRA contributions made with after-tax dollars and are not deductible.

##### SIMPLE IRAs: Traditional and Roth

A SIMPLE IRA plan is an employer-sponsored plan under which certain small employers (including self-employed individuals) can make contributions to traditional SIMPLE IRAs or Roth SIMPLE IRAs for their employees. Employer contributions under a SIMPLE IRA plan do not prevent individuals from making personal contributions to separate traditional or Roth IRAs. Individuals cannot make personal contributions to their SIMPLE IRAs; only employers contribute.

##### Special Terms

**Certain 2025 retirement plan distribution repayments treated as rollovers.** This term describes repayments of distributions made in 2025:

- Qualified disaster distributions
- Qualified birth or adoption distributions
- Emergency personal expense distributions
- Domestic abuse distributions
- Terminal illness distributions

These repayments receive special rollover treatment when made within designated repayment periods.

**Any 2025 retirement plan distributions whose repayments are treated as rollovers.** This term describes distributions made in 2025:

- Qualified disaster distributions
- Qualified birth or adoption distributions
- Emergency personal expense distributions
- Domestic abuse distributions
- Terminal illness distributions
