#### Overall Contribution Limit for Traditional and Roth IRAs

For most filers, the overall contribution limit for traditional and Roth IRAs combined is the smaller of:

- $7,000 ($8,000 if age 50 or older at the end of 2025), or
- Your taxable compensation for the year.

If married filing jointly, each spouse has a separate limit of $7,000 ($8,000 if age 50 or older at the end of 2025).

This limit does not apply to employer contributions to traditional SEP IRAs, traditional SIMPLE IRAs, Roth SEP IRAs, or Roth SIMPLE IRAs.

Rollovers, Roth IRA conversions, and repayments of qualified reservist distributions and certain other distributions do not count toward the contribution limit.

The amount you can contribute to a Roth IRA may also be reduced based on your modified AGI. See the Maximum Roth IRA Contribution Worksheet, earlier.

**Difficulty of care payments.** For 2025 contributions, you may elect to increase the nondeductible IRA contribution limit by some or all difficulty of care payments received (qualified foster care payments). See 2025 Publication 590-A for details.

**Taxable compensation.** Taxable compensation includes:

- Wages, salaries, tips, professional fees, bonuses, and other amounts received for providing personal services. However, taxable compensation doesn't include amounts received from nonqualified deferred compensation plans or nongovernmental section 457 plans shown in specific boxes on Form W-2 or Form 1099-NEC.
- Nontaxable combat pay if you were a member of the U.S. Armed Forces.
- Self-employment income (net earnings from self-employment reduced by the deduction for contributions to retirement plans for self-employed and the deduction for one-half of self-employment tax).
- Alimony and separate maintenance payments from divorce or separation agreements executed before January 1, 2019 (unless the agreement was modified after December 31, 2018, to expressly provide that alimony payments are not includible in the recipient's income).
- Certain non-tuition fellowship and stipend payments.

#### Recharacterizations

Generally, you can recharacterize an IRA contribution by making a trustee-to-trustee transfer of a contribution from one type of IRA to another. You must generally make the transfer by the due date of your return (including extensions) and reflect it on your return. If you timely filed your return without making the transfer, you can still make it within 6 months of the due date of your return, excluding extensions. File an amended return with "Filed pursuant to section 301.9100-2" written at the top.

**No recharacterizations of conversions made in 2018 or later.** A conversion of a traditional IRA to a Roth IRA, and a rollover from any other eligible retirement plan to a Roth IRA, made in tax years beginning after December 31, 2017, cannot be recharacterized as having been made to a traditional IRA.

**Reporting recharacterizations.** Treat a recharacterized IRA contribution as if it was originally contributed to the second IRA (the IRA to which you transferred the contribution). The transfer must include the original contribution plus any related earnings (or minus any related losses). Treat the earnings or losses as having occurred in the second IRA. You cannot deduct a net loss that occurs in the first IRA, and you cannot take a deduction for a contribution you later recharacterize.

There are two types of recharacterizations:

1. **Traditional IRA contribution recharacterized as a Roth IRA contribution.** Report any remaining nondeductible portion of the traditional IRA contribution on Form 8606, Part I. If the entire contribution was recharacterized, don't report it on Form 8606. Attach a statement explaining the recharacterization.

2. **Roth IRA contribution recharacterized as a traditional IRA contribution.** Report the nondeductible portion of the recharacterized traditional IRA contribution on Form 8606, Part I. Don't report the original Roth IRA contribution on Form 8606. Attach a statement explaining the recharacterization.

#### Return of IRA Contributions

If your 2025 IRA contributions are returned to you with any related earnings (or minus any related loss) by the due date (including extensions) of your 2025 return, the contributions are treated as if they were never made. Don't report the contribution or the distribution on Form 8606 or take a deduction for the contribution. However, you must include the distribution on Form 1040, 1040-SR, or 1040-NR, line 4a, and include any net income (earnings) on line 4b. Attach a statement to your return explaining the distribution.

If you were under age 59-1/2 at the time of the distribution, you are generally subject to the additional 10% tax on early distributions.

If you timely filed your 2025 return without withdrawing a 2025 contribution, you can still have the contribution returned to you within 6 months of the due date of your return, excluding extensions. File an amended return with "Filed pursuant to section 301.9100-2" written at the top, and report the earnings on the amended return. Attach an explanation to the amended return.

For contributions made for 2024 and returned in 2025, report the distribution on your 2024 original or amended return.

#### Return of Excess Traditional IRA Contributions

The return of excess traditional IRA contributions for years before 2025 isn't taxable if all three of the following conditions are met.

1. The distribution was made after the due date (including extensions) of your return for the year the excess contribution was made.
2. No deduction was allowed or taken for the excess contributions for the contribution year.
3. The total contributions (other than employer contributions to a SEP IRA and rollover contributions) to the IRA for the year of the excess contribution didn't exceed the applicable limit.

**Contribution Limits by Year:**

| Year(s) | Limit (Under 50) | Limit (Age 50 or Older) |
|---------|-------------------|-------------------------|
| 2024 | $7,000 | $8,000 |
| 2023 | $6,500 | $7,500 |
| 2019-2022 | $6,000 | $7,000 |
| 2013-2018 | $5,500 | $6,500 |
| 2008-2012 | $5,000 | $6,000 |
| 2006-2007 | $4,000 | $5,000 |
| 2005 | $4,000 | $4,500 |
| 2002-2004 | $3,000 | $3,500 |
| 1997-2001 | $2,000 | N/A |
| Before 1997 | $2,250 | N/A |

If any part of the excess contribution for a prior year included a rollover contribution, and the excess was due to incorrect information supplied by the plan making the distribution, increase the contribution limit shown above by the amount of the excess attributable to the incorrect information.

If the contributions for the year of the excess included employer contributions to a traditional SEP IRA under a SEP arrangement, increase the limit above by the smaller of: (a) the employer's contribution to the SEP IRA, or (b) the applicable annual limit for employer contributions ($30,000 for years before 2001; $35,000 for 2001; $40,000 for 2002-2003; $41,000 for 2004; $42,000 for 2005; $44,000 for 2006; $45,000 for 2007; $46,000 for 2008-2009; $49,000 for 2010-2011; $50,000 for 2012; $51,000 for 2013; $52,000 for 2014-2015; $53,000 for 2016; $54,000 for 2017; $55,000 for 2018; $56,000 for 2019; $57,000 for 2020; $58,000 for 2021; $61,000 for 2022; $66,000 for 2023; $69,000 for 2024).

Include the total distributed amount on Form 1040, 1040-SR, or 1040-NR, line 4a, and attach a statement explaining the distribution.

If you are otherwise required to file Form 8606 and met the above conditions:

- Don't include the withdrawn contributions in figuring your basis on line 2 (for 2025 or later years).
- Don't include the withdrawn contributions on line 7.

#### Amending Form 8606

After filing your return, you can change a nondeductible traditional IRA contribution to a deductible contribution, or vice versa, if the change is made within the time limit for filing Form 1040-X (generally within 3 years after the date you filed your original return or within 2 years after the date you paid the tax, whichever is later). You may also make a recharacterization within this period. If you need to amend, complete a new Form 8606 showing the revised information and file it with Form 1040-X.

#### Penalty for Not Filing

If you are required to file Form 8606 to report a nondeductible contribution to a traditional IRA for 2025 and you fail to do so, you must pay a $50 penalty unless you can show reasonable cause.

#### Overstatement Penalty

If you overstate your nondeductible contributions on Form 8606 for any taxable year, you must pay a penalty of $100 for each overstatement unless you can show reasonable cause.

#### What Records Must I Keep?

To verify the nontaxable portion of your IRA distributions, you must keep copies of the following forms and records until all distributions from your traditional and Roth IRAs are complete:

- Page 1 of your Forms 1040 or 1040-SR filed for each year you made a nondeductible traditional IRA contribution.
- Forms 8606 filed for all applicable years, including any supporting statements, attachments, and worksheets.
- Forms 5498 or similar statements received each year showing contributions to traditional or Roth IRAs.
- Forms 5498 or similar statements received each year showing the December 31 value of traditional IRAs for each year you received a distribution.
- Forms 1099-R (or W-2P for earlier years) received for each year you received a distribution from an IRA.

**Note:** Forms 1040-T, 1040A, and W-2P are forms used in prior years but may still be relevant for historical records.
