### General Instructions

#### Purpose of Form

Use Form 8995 to figure your qualified business income (QBI) deduction. Form 8995 enables eligible taxpayers to calculate a deduction of up to 20% of qualified business income (QBI) from trades or businesses, plus 20% of qualified REIT dividends and qualified PTP income, though the total deduction cannot exceed 20% of taxable income minus net capital gains.

#### Who Can Use Form 8995

You can use Form 8995 if you have QBI, qualified REIT dividends, or qualified PTP income or loss, and your 2025 taxable income before the QBI deduction is at or below:

  * $394,600 if married filing jointly
  * $197,300 for all other filing statuses

Taxpayers exceeding these thresholds must use Form 8995-A instead.

Eligible filers include:

  * Individuals with QBI, qualified REIT dividends, or qualified PTP income/loss
  * Eligible estates and trusts

You cannot use Form 8995 if you are a patron in a specified agricultural or horticultural cooperative.

**Note.** S corporations, partnerships, and regular C corporations cannot claim the deduction themselves but must pass information to owners/shareholders.

#### Qualified Trades or Businesses (QTB)

##### Definition

A qualified trade or business (QTB) is a domestic trade or business that allows ordinary and necessary business expense deductions under section 162 of the Internal Revenue Code. The following are excluded:

  * Corporate-operated businesses
  * Services performed as an employee (with limited exceptions for statutory employees)
  * Most specified service trades or businesses (SSTBs)

##### Specified Service Trade or Business (SSTB)

An SSTB is a trade or business providing services in:

  * Health
  * Law
  * Accounting
  * Actuarial science
  * Performing arts
  * Consulting
  * Athletics
  * Financial services
  * Brokerage services
  * Investing and investment management
  * Trading or dealing in securities, partnership interests, or commodities
  * Any trade or business where the principal asset is the reputation or skill of one or more of its employees or owners (demonstrated through endorsement deals, licensing of an individual's image, name, or signature, or appearance fees)

##### SSTB Exceptions

**Exception 1.** If your 2025 taxable income is at or below the threshold ($394,600 MFJ / $197,300 others), SSTBs qualify as QTBs.

**Exception 2.** If income exceeds the threshold but falls within the phase-in range ($394,601--$494,600 MFJ / $197,301--$247,300 others), an applicable percentage of SSTB income qualifies.

##### Real Property and Rental Activities

Ownership and rental of real property may constitute a trade or business if the primary purpose is income or profit and it is conducted with continuity and regularity. Rev. Proc. 2019-38 provides a safe harbor treating qualifying rental real estate enterprises as trades or businesses for QBI purposes.

##### Aggregation Rules

Multiple trades or businesses can be aggregated into a single QTB if:

1. You (or a group) directly or indirectly own 50% or more of each business for the majority of the tax year (including the last day of the tax year), and all businesses share the same tax year-end.
2. None of the businesses are SSTBs.
3. The businesses meet at least two of the following factors:
   * They provide the same products, property, or services, or products, property, or services that are customarily offered together.
   * They share facilities or significant centralized business elements (such as personnel, accounting, legal, manufacturing, purchasing, HR, or IT resources).
   * They operate in coordination with, or reliance upon, one or more of the other aggregated businesses.

**Note.** Combined QBI, W-2 wages, and UBIA of qualified property must be tracked for aggregated businesses. These limits apply only when income exceeds the threshold (requiring Form 8995-A).

#### Determining Your Qualified Business Income

##### What Is Included in QBI

QBI comprises qualified items of income, gain, deduction, and loss from qualified trades or businesses effectively connected with U.S. business operations. Items include:

  * Unreimbursed partnership expenses
  * Business interest expense
  * Deductible self-employment tax portion
  * Self-employment health insurance deduction
  * Qualified retirement plan contributions

**CRITICAL — QBI is NOT simply net profit from Schedule C or Schedule F.** The deduction items above *reduce* QBI. For a self-employed taxpayer filing Schedule C, QBI for that business equals:

  Schedule C line 31 (net profit)
  MINUS deductible part of SE tax (Schedule SE line 13, allocated to this business)
  MINUS self-employed health insurance deduction (Schedule 1 line 17, if attributable to this business)
  MINUS contributions to qualified retirement plans (Schedule 1 line 16, if attributable to this business)

For MFJ filers with multiple businesses, allocate the SE tax deduction proportionally to each business based on its share of total net SE earnings. Enter the adjusted QBI (after these reductions) on Form 8995 line 1(c).

##### What Is Excluded from QBI

  * Items improperly included in income
  * Non-U.S.-connected income
  * Wage income reported on Form W-2, box 1 (except statutory employees per Form W-2, box 13)
  * Reasonable compensation paid by an S corporation
  * Guaranteed payments to partners for services rendered
  * Payments to a partner for services outside the capacity of being a partner
  * Capital gains or losses
  * Dividends and dividend equivalents
  * Interest income not allocable to the trade or business
  * Commodities transactions or foreign currency gains or losses
  * Notional principal contract income, loss, or deductions
  * Annuities (unless received in connection with the trade or business)
  * Qualified REIT dividends
  * Qualified PTP income
  * Tip income excluded from business net profit under section 224

##### Losses and Deductions Suspended by Other Provisions

Losses or deductions suspended under other provisions of the Internal Revenue Code (sections 163(j), 179, 461(l), 465, 469, 704(d), 1366(d)) are NOT included in QBI in the year of suspension. They are included in QBI only when subsequently allowed in calculating taxable income.

**Pre-2018 losses.** Generally, losses incurred before 2018 are not treated as qualified losses or deductions and are not included in QBI when allowed.

**Partial suspensions.** For losses that are only partially suspended, determine the QBI portion by calculating the percentage of the total loss attributable to QBI, then multiply the allowed loss by this percentage.

**SSTB status.** The determination of whether a loss or deduction is from an SSTB is made in the year the loss or deduction occurs. If taxable income falls within the phase-in range in that year, apply the applicable percentage in that year to determine the qualified suspended portion.

##### Loss Tracking

Losses and deductions retain their qualified or non-qualified status across years while suspended. A QBI Loss Tracking Worksheet (see below) helps monitor suspended losses separately for each suspending provision.

When previously suspended losses are allowed, the qualified portion is treated as a qualified net loss carryforward from a separate trade or business on Line 3 of Form 8995.

#### Schedule K-1 Items (From Partnerships, S Corporations, Estates, Trusts)

Amounts reported as "QBI/Qualified PTP Items Subject to Taxpayer-Specific Determinations" on Schedule K-1 are NOT automatically included in QBI. You must determine inclusion by referencing how the item appears on your federal income tax return. Ordinary business income or loss is generally includible if it is used in computing taxable income and is not excluded, suspended, or disallowed under another code section.

Use the QBI Flow Chart (included in the instructions) to verify includibility.

#### Form 1099-PATR (Cooperative Patronage Dividends)

Amounts reported on Form 1099-PATR are not automatically included in QBI. Inclusion requires:

1. The amounts are related to your trade or business.
2. They are reported as qualified income items by the cooperative on an attachment to Form 1099-PATR.
3. They are not from an SSTB (or they are from an SSTB and your taxable income is at or below the threshold).

If you received qualified payments from a specified agricultural or horticultural cooperative, reduce QBI by the patron reduction and use Form 8995-A to compute your deduction.

#### Schedule C Items (Self-Employed Income)

Net gain or loss on Schedule C (Form 1040) is not automatically included in QBI. Use the QBI Flow Chart to determine includibility.

#### Qualified REIT Dividends

Qualified REIT dividends include dividends from REITs held longer than 45 days where:

  * The payment is not obligated to another person.
  * The dividend is not a capital gain dividend or a qualified dividend.
  * Plus, qualified REIT dividends received from regulated investment companies (RICs).

This amount is reported on Form 1099-DIV, line 5.

#### Qualified PTP Income/Loss

Qualified PTP income or loss includes your share of qualified items from publicly traded partnerships (PTPs) not taxed as corporations, plus any gain or loss on the disposition of a partnership interest that is not treated as a capital gain or loss.

**Important.** PTP income from SSTBs may be limited to an applicable percentage or excluded entirely if your taxable income exceeds the threshold, potentially requiring Schedule A (Form 8995-A).

Suspended PTP losses must be tracked separately. When allowed, the qualified portion becomes a qualified net loss carryforward from a separate PTP on Line 7 of Form 8995. Pre-2018 losses are generally not qualified. If the PTP is an SSTB, the determination is made in the year the loss occurs; within the phase-in range, apply the applicable percentage in that year.
