#### Items for Special Treatment

The following items receive special treatment:

**Securities dealer transactions.** Follow section 475 and related revenue rulings.

**Bonds and debt instruments.** See Publication 550.

**Real estate subdivisions.** Property potentially qualifying as capital assets: see section 1237.

**Depreciated property sales to more-than-50%-owned entities or beneficiary trusts.** May generate recapture income.

**Domestic International Sales Corporation (DISC) stock.** Gains follow section 995(c).

**Foreign corporation stock.** See section 1248.

**Partnership property transfers potentially classified as investment companies.** See Publication 541.

**Utility dividend reinvestment plan shares.** See Publication 550.

**Political organization property transfers.** Follow section 84.

**Foreign estate/trust property transfers.** See section 684.

**U.S. citizenship renunciation.** Triggers deemed property sales at fair market value. Long-term permanent residents ceasing residency face similar treatment. See Publications 519 and Form 8854.

**Divorce property transfers.** Between spouses incident to divorce receive non-recognition treatment. See Publication 504.

**Debt retirement.** Generally triggers exchange treatment. See Publication 550.

**Converted wetland/erodible cropland.** Losses report as long-term capital losses on Form 8949; gains report as ordinary income on Form 4797.

**Extraordinary dividends.** Create long-term capital loss limitations when stock sales follow extraordinary dividends equaling or exceeding 10% of basis (5% for preferred stock).

**Corporate liquidation distributions.** See Publication 550.

**Fractional stock cash from splits or dividends.** See Publication 550.

**Regulated investment company load charges.** May not reduce gains on dispositions with reinvestment rights. See Publication 550.

**Partnership/S corporation/trust interest sales exceeding one year.** May generate 28% rate collectibles gains.

**Securities futures contracts.** See Publication 550.

**Appreciated financial position constructive sales.** Generate capital gain. See Publication 550.

**Constructive ownership transactions (section 1260).** Create ordinary income treatment for gains exceeding amounts that would have been recognized if assets were held directly. Interest accrues on open positions from prior years, reported on Schedule 2 (Form 1040), line 17z with "Section 1260(b) interest" notation. This interest is non-deductible.

**Investment club stock.** See Publication 550.

**Virtual currencies (Bitcoin, etc.).** See Form 1040 instructions and IRS FAQs.

**Qualified Opportunity Fund deferrals.** Report on normal gain forms with deferral on Form 8949. See Form 8997 instructions.

#### Market Discount Bonds

When you dispose of a market discount bond, gain is treated as interest income to the extent of the accrued market discount at the time of disposition. See sections 1276 through 1278 and Publication 550 for details. Form 8949 instructions provide specific reporting guidance.

#### Contingent Payment Debt Instruments

Gain from the sale of a contingent payment debt instrument is treated as interest income rather than capital gain under the noncontingent bond method. Loss on such instruments attributable to original issue discount inclusions is treated as ordinary loss; any remaining loss is capital loss if the instrument is a capital asset.

If Form 1099-B or Form 1099-DA reports the transaction with the "Ordinary" box checked, you may need adjustments. Use Form 8949's contingent payment debt instrument adjustment worksheet to calculate the required column (g) entry. See Publications 550 and 1212.

#### Wash Sales

A wash sale occurs when you sell stock or securities at a loss and, within 30 days before or after the sale, you:

* Buy substantially identical stock or securities,
* Acquire substantially identical stock or securities in a taxable trade,
* Acquire a contract or option to buy substantially identical stock or securities, or
* Acquire substantially identical stock or securities for your IRA or Roth IRA.

The loss from a wash sale is not deductible unless you are a dealer in stock or securities. The disallowed loss is added to the basis of the replacement property (except for IRA acquisitions).

Floating-NAV money market fund redemptions are not subject to wash sale rules.

Tokenized securities wash sale rules generally apply to digital assets classified as stock or securities for tax purposes.

Form 1099-B box 1g or Form 1099-DA box 1i typically shows nondeductible wash sale losses when the sold stock was a "covered security," the replacement securities shared the same CUSIP number, and the same account held both transactions. However, wash sale losses remain non-deductible even if not reported on Forms 1099-B or 1099-DA.

See Publication 550 for comprehensive wash sale rules.

Report wash sales on Form 8949 by completing all columns, entering "W" in column (f), the disallowed loss as a positive number in column (g), and completing column (h) per instructions.

#### Traders in Securities

A trader in securities is a person engaged in buying and selling securities for their own account. To be considered a trader, you must meet all three criteria:

1. You must seek to profit from daily market price movements, not from dividends, interest, or capital appreciation.
2. Your activity must be substantial.
3. You must carry on the activity with continuity and regularity.

Factors considered include typical holding periods, frequency and dollar amount of trades, extent of income-producing motivation, and time devoted to the activity.

If you do not meet these criteria, you are an investor, regardless of whether you call yourself a "trader" or "day trader."

Like investors, traders report securities transactions on Form 8949 and Schedule D, unless a mark-to-market election applies. Regardless of reporting form, trader security gains/losses are excluded from self-employment tax calculations (with limited exceptions for section 1256 contracts).

Trading business interest expense is reported on Schedule C, not Schedule A. Investment interest limitations do not apply to trading businesses. Traders may allocate expenses between trading and investment securities.

#### Mark-to-Market Election for Traders

Under section 475(f), traders can elect to treat all securities gains and losses as ordinary income, including year-end holdings treated as sold at fair market value on the last business day of the year.

The election must be made by the due date (without regard to extensions) of the tax return for the year before the election year. For 2025 effectiveness, the election had to be made by the 2024 return due date.

Once the election is in effect, report all trading business security gains and losses on Form 4797, Part II, including year-end mark-to-market amounts. Securities held for investment must be separately identified and are not subject to mark-to-market treatment.

See Publication 550 and Revenue Procedure 99-17 for election procedures.

#### Short Sales

A short sale involves selling borrowed property (usually securities) with a commitment to return identical property later. Gain or loss equals the difference between the sale price and the cost of replacement property.

**Holding period rules.** The holding period generally reflects how long you actually held property. However:

* If you held substantially identical property for one year or less at the time of the short sale, any gain on closing is short-term.
* If you acquired substantially identical property after the short sale date but before closing, any gain is short-term.
* If you held substantially identical property for more than one year at the time of the short sale, any loss on closing is long-term, regardless of how long you held the replacement property.

**Reporting.** Report short sales on Form 8949 in the year the sale closes.

Pre-2011 short sales closed in 2025 without a 2025 Form 1099-B or Form 1099-DA: report in Part I (box C or I) or Part II (box F or L) with descriptive notation.

#### Gain or Loss From Options

Report gains and losses from options on capital assets on Form 8949, except section 1256 contracts (use Form 6781).

**Expired purchased options.** Enter the expiration date in column (c) and "EXPIRED" in column (d).

**Expired written options.** Enter the expiration date in column (b) and "EXPIRED" in column (e).

**Post-2013 sold call options exercised.** The premium is reflected in Form 1099-B box 1d or Form 1099-DA box 1f as part of the proceeds.

**Pre-2014 option premiums.** May require a manual entry as a positive number in column (g) with "E" in column (f).

**Example.** In 2013, you sold a $10 call option on XYZ stock that was later exercised. Enter $80 in column (d), "E" in column (f), $10 in column (g), and complete remaining columns per instructions.

#### Net Asset Value (NAV) Method for Money Market Funds

Capital gains or losses from NAV method money market funds are reported on Form 8949, Part I, with box C or I checked. Enter the fund name followed by "(NAV)" in column (a), the net gain or loss in column (h), and leave other columns blank.

#### Undistributed Capital Gains

Enter the amount from Form 2439, box 1a (undistributed long-term capital gains) on Schedule D, line 11.

**Special Form 2439 boxes:**

* Box 1b: Include on Unrecaptured Section 1250 Gain Worksheet, line 11
* Box 1c: May qualify for section 1202 exclusion
* Box 1d: Include on 28% Rate Gain Worksheet, line 4

Enter the tax shown in Form 2439, box 2, on Schedule 3 (Form 1040), line 13a. Increase your stock basis by the difference between the gain (box 1a) and the tax (box 2). See Publication 550.

#### Installment Sales

Property sales (excluding publicly traded stocks and securities) at a gain with payments in later years must be reported on Form 6252 under the installment method, unless you elect otherwise on a timely filed return.

Also use Form 6252 to report 2025 payments from earlier-year installment sales.

To elect out of the installment method, report the full gain on Form 8949 for the sale year. Original-return elections on amended returns must be filed within 6 months of the return due date (excluding extensions) with the notation "Filed pursuant to section 301.9100-2."

#### Demutualization of Life Insurance Companies

When a mutual life insurance company converts to a stock company:

**Tax-free reorganizations.** No gain or loss on exchange of equity interests for stock. Holding periods include mutual company equity-interest periods. Cash received in equity-interest exchange generates capital gain (long-term if equity interests were long-term; short-term otherwise). Report on Form 8949.

**Non-qualifying transactions.** Capital gain or loss is recognized. Holding periods begin the day after receipt. Report on Form 8949.
