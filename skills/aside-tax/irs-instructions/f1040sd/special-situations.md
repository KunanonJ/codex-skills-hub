#### Capital Gain Distributions

Mutual funds and REITs pay capital gain distributions from net realized long-term capital gains. Short-term capital gain distributions are reported as ordinary dividends on Form 1099-DIV, not as capital gain distributions.

Enter the total capital gain distributions on Schedule D, line 13, regardless of holding period. This amount appears in Form 1099-DIV, box 2a.

**Special distribution amounts:**

* Box 2b (Unrecaptured section 1250 gain): Include on Unrecaptured Section 1250 Gain Worksheet, line 11
* Box 2c (Section 1202 gain): May qualify for section 1202 QSB stock exclusion
* Box 2d (Collectibles 28% rate gain): Include on 28% Rate Gain Worksheet, line 4

If you received capital gain distributions as a nominee (in your name but actually belonging to someone else), report only your share on Schedule D, line 13. Attach a statement showing the full amount you received and the amount of the other person's share.

#### Sale of Your Home

You may not need to report the sale of your home on Schedule D or Form 8949 if you can exclude all of the gain from income and you did not receive a Form 1099-S.

**Two-part exclusion test:**

**Test 1 (Ownership and Use Test).** During the 5-year period ending on the date of the sale, you owned the home for at least 2 years and lived in it as your main home for at least 2 years.

**Test 2 (Prior Sale Test).** During the 2-year period ending on the date of the sale, you did not exclude gain from the sale of another home.

If you meet both tests, you can exclude up to $250,000 of gain ($500,000 if married filing jointly). For married filing jointly, only one spouse needs to meet the ownership requirement, but both must meet the use requirement.

**Reduced exclusion.** If you sold your home due to a change in employment, health, or unforeseen circumstances, you may qualify for a reduced exclusion even if you do not meet both tests.

**Surviving spouse provision.** A surviving spouse may exclude up to $500,000 of gain if:

* The sale occurs within 2 years of the spouse's death
* Both spouses met the use requirement; at least one met the ownership requirement
* Both met Test 2 at the time of the spouse's death
* The surviving spouse did not remarry before the sale

**Qualified extended duty exception.** The 5-year test period may be suspended for military service, Foreign Service employment, Peace Corps volunteering, intelligence community work, or other qualified official extended duty. The suspension cannot exceed 10 years.

**Example.** You purchased a home in Virginia in 2013, lived in it for 3 years, then served on active military duty in Kuwait for 8 years. Under the suspension election, the 5-year test period is extended, and you can qualify for the exclusion when you sell in 2025.

**Qualified extended duty definition.** Qualified extended duty requires:

* Active duty call or order for indefinite period or exceeding 90 days
* Duty station at least 50 miles from main home, or occupancy of government quarters

**Like-kind exchange limitation.** If you acquired your home through a like-kind exchange, you cannot exclude gain if the sale occurs within 5 years of acquisition.

**Reporting home sales.** Report on Form 8949. Short-term transactions go in Part I (check box C or I); long-term in Part II (check box F or L). To exclude part or all of gain, enter "H" in column (f) and the exclusion as a negative number in column (g).

If you had a loss on a home sale reported on Form 1099-S, see the Certain Nondeductible Losses section.

See Publication 523 for additional guidance, including rules on business/rental use and periods of non-primary residence.

#### Partnership Interests

Sales of partnership interests may produce ordinary income, collectibles gain (28% rate), or unrecaptured section 1250 gain. See the instructions for line 18 (28% rate gain) and line 19 (unrecaptured section 1250 gain).

#### Capital Assets Held for Personal Use

If you sell a capital asset held for personal use at a gain, you must report the gain. However, if you sell it at a loss, you generally cannot deduct the loss. Exceptions apply when you receive Form 1099-S (real estate) or Form 1099-K (personal property), in which case you must report the transaction even though the loss is not deductible.

If you converted personal-use property to business or rental use and then sold it, any depreciation recapture is reported as ordinary income on Form 4797, Part III.

**Example (non-deductible loss on personal-use property).** You sell a vacation home at a loss and receive Form 1099-S. Report on Form 8949: enter the loss amount, mark "L" in column (f), enter the loss difference as a positive number in column (g), and show -0- in column (h).

#### Capital Losses

You can deduct capital losses up to the amount of your capital gains plus $3,000 ($1,500 if married filing separately). Excess losses carry forward to future years indefinitely. You must report all capital gains and losses even if losses cannot be fully used in 2025.

#### Certain Nondeductible Losses

Losses between certain related parties are not deductible. Related parties include:

* Family members (spouses, siblings, parents, children, grandchildren)
* A corporation and an individual owning more than 50% of its stock (except in complete liquidations)
* Grantors and trust fiduciaries
* Fiduciaries and trust beneficiaries
* Fiduciaries of different trusts with the same grantor
* Estate executors and beneficiaries (except for sales to satisfy pecuniary bequests)
* Individuals and tax-exempt organizations they control

Report nondeductible losses on Form 8949 by checking appropriate boxes, entering "L" in column (f), the nondeductible loss as a positive number in column (g), and -0- in column (h).

**Example 1.** You sold land to your brother at a loss. Enter the selling price and basis normally, mark "L" in column (f), enter the loss amount as a positive number in column (g), and -0- in column (h).

**Example 2.** A Form 1099-B or Form 1099-DA reports a loss, and box 7 (Form 1099-B) or box 5 (Form 1099-DA) indicates the loss is disallowed. Enter appropriate box codes and amounts, "L" in column (f), the loss difference as a positive number in column (g), and -0- in column (h).

**At-risk rules.** If you dispose of assets in at-risk activities or activity interests with amounts at risk, see the Instructions for Form 6198.

**Passive activity rules.** Capital losses subject to at-risk rules may also face passive activity limitations. See Form 8582 for detailed guidance.
