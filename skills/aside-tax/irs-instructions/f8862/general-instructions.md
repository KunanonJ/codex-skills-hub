### General Instructions

#### Purpose of Form

You must complete Form 8862 and attach it to your tax return to claim the EIC, CTC, ACTC, ODC, or AOTC if you meet these criteria:

- **Your EIC** for a year after 1996 was reduced or disallowed for any reason other than a math or clerical error, you now meet all requirements, and you want to take the EIC.
- **Your CTC, RCTC, ACTC, or ODC** for a year after 2015 was reduced or disallowed for any reason other than a math or clerical error, you now meet all requirements, and you want to take these credits.
- **Your AOTC** for a year after 2015 was reduced or disallowed for any reason other than a math or clerical error, you now meet all requirements, and you want to take the AOTC.

*Note: The refundable child tax credit (RCTC) was only available for 2021.*

You must attach applicable schedules and forms to your return for each credit claimed. The IRS may request additional information before issuing any refund.

#### Exceptions — When NOT to File Form 8862

Do **not** file Form 8862 if:

1. After your EIC, CTC/ACTC/ODC, or AOTC was reduced or disallowed in an earlier year: (a) you filed Form 8862 (or other documents) and your credit was then allowed, **and** (b) your credit has not been reduced or disallowed again for any reason other than a math or clerical error.

2. You are claiming the EIC without a qualifying child and the only reason your EIC was reduced or disallowed was because a child on Schedule EIC was determined not to be your qualifying child.

In these cases, you can claim the credit(s) without Form 8862 if you meet all eligibility requirements.

#### Ban Periods — When You Cannot File

Do **not** file Form 8862 for the:

- **2 years** after the most recent tax year for which there was a final determination that your EIC, CTC/ACTC/ODC, or AOTC claim was due to **reckless or intentional disregard** of the rules.
- **10 years** after the most recent tax year for which there was a final determination that your EIC, CTC/ACTC/ODC, or AOTC claim was due to **fraud**.

In these cases, the final determination means you cannot claim the credit(s). However, if you disagree with the final determination and want to appeal the 2- or 10-year disallowance period, file Form 8862 and follow the appeal instructions below.

#### How to Appeal the Disallowance Period

You can request reconsideration of the ban by sending documentation proving entitlement to the credits for the year the ban was imposed, or documentation showing the claim was not due to recklessness, intentional disregard (2-year ban) or fraud (10-year ban).

You can contest the ban in U.S. Tax Court, but because the ban impacts eligibility in years after it is imposed, you must wait until filing your return for a later tax year within the ban period when you would otherwise be eligible for the credit (ignoring the ban). When claiming the credit during the ban, attach Form 8862 and applicable schedules and forms to your return. **Mail this return to the IRS** because e-filed returns attempting to claim credits during a ban period will be rejected.

**Example:** You claimed the EIC on your 2024 return filed in March 2025. The IRS determined you were not entitled to the EIC and that the error resulted from reckless or intentional disregard of EIC rules. In September 2025, you received a statutory notice of deficiency. You did not petition Tax Court within 90 days, so your EIC was denied in December 2025. You received a CP79A notice in January 2026 stating you cannot claim the EIC for 2025 and 2026. If you would be eligible for the EIC for 2025 without the ban, claim it with an attached Form 8862 on your 2025 return to appeal the ban. After filing, you will receive a notice disallowing the credit due to the ban. Follow instructions in that notice to challenge both the ban and credit disallowance.

See Publication 5, *Your Appeal Rights and How To Prepare a Protest if You Disagree*, for information on filing an appeal with the courts.

#### Have an ITIN Instead of an SSN?

You must have a valid SSN to claim the EIC. An SSN is valid for EIC unless issued after the return due date (including extensions) or issued solely for federal benefit applications without work authorization.

Beginning in 2025, you must also have a valid SSN to claim CTC or ACTC. An SSN is valid for these credits if it is valid for employment and issued before the return due date (including extensions). On joint returns, only one spouse needs a valid SSN for CTC or ACTC; the other must have an SSN or ITIN issued by the due date.

You can have an ITIN instead of an SSN to claim the ODC or AOTC, or to claim CTC or ACTC for a 2024 or earlier return. The ITIN must have been issued by the return due date (including extensions). If you have an ITIN, enter it in the social security number space on the form.

#### Need More Space for an Item?

If you need more space, attach a statement to your return's end including your name and SSN (or ITIN). Number each entry on the statement to correspond with Form 8862 line numbers.
