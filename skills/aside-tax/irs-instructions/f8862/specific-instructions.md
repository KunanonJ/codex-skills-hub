### Specific Instructions

#### Part I — All Filers

**Line 1.** Enter the year for which you are filing this form to claim the credit(s) (for example, 2025). Do not enter the year the credit(s) was disallowed.

**Line 2.** Check the box(es) for the credit(s) you are now claiming. You can check a box for each credit previously reduced or disallowed.

---

#### Part II — Earned Income Credit

**Line 3.** If the **only** reason your EIC was reduced or disallowed was because you incorrectly reported your earned income or investment income, check "Yes." Otherwise, check "No."

**Caution:** If you checked "Yes," do not complete the rest of Part II. Attach this form to your tax return to claim the EIC. If you checked "No," continue.

**Line 4.** If you (or your spouse if filing jointly) were a qualifying child of another taxpayer for the year shown on line 1, you cannot claim the EIC. Check "Yes" on line 4. This is true even if the taxpayer for whom you (or your spouse) are a qualifying child does not claim the EIC or meet all rules to claim it. See Publication 596, *Earned Income Credit*.

Check "No" on line 4 if you could be claimed as a qualifying child of another taxpayer for the year on line 1, but that taxpayer is not required to file and is not filing a return for that year, or is filing only to claim a refund of withheld income tax or estimated tax paid.

**Caution:** If you (or your spouse if filing jointly) answer "Yes" to question 4, you cannot claim the EIC.

##### Section A: Filers With a Qualifying Child or Children

If you are married but not filing jointly and have a qualifying child, you may qualify to claim the EIC. See Lines 27a, 27b, and 27c in Form 1040 Instructions and Schedule EIC (Form 1040).

**Line 6.** A qualifying child for the EIC is a child who:

1. Is your son, daughter, stepchild, foster child, brother, sister, stepbrother, stepsister, half-brother, half-sister, or a descendant of any of them (for example, grandchild, niece, or nephew).
2. Was (a) under age 19 at year-end and younger than you (or your spouse if filing jointly), (b) under age 24 at year-end, a student, and younger than you (or your spouse), or (c) any age and permanently and totally disabled.
3. Lived with you in the United States for more than half the year on line 1.
4. Meets the joint return test: Your child meets this test if not filing a joint return with their spouse for the year on line 1, or filing a joint return only to claim a refund of withheld income tax or estimated tax paid.

**Adopted Child:** An adopted child is always treated as your own child. This includes a child lawfully placed with you for legal adoption.

See your tax return instructions or Publication 596 for more information on qualifying children.

**Line 7.** You cannot claim the credit for a child who lived with you for half the year or less, even if you paid most living expenses. The IRS may request documents showing you lived with each qualifying child. Keep school and child care records and other documents showing your child's address.

Temporary absences for special circumstances (school, vacation, business, medical care, military service, or juvenile facility detention) count as time the child lived with you.

If your child was born or died during the year on line 1 and your home was the child's home for more than half the time the child was alive that year, enter "365" ("366" for leap years) on line 7 and complete line 8.

See your tax return instructions or Publication 596 for more information.

##### Section B: Filers Without a Qualifying Child or Children

**Line 9.** Enter the number of days your main home (and your spouse's if filing jointly) was in the United States during the year on line 1.

Temporary absences for special circumstances (business, medical care, school, or military service) count as time at your main home.

**Members of the Military:** If you were on extended active duty outside the United States, your main home is considered to be in the United States during that duty period. Extended active duty is military duty ordered for an indefinite period or for more than 90 days. Once you begin serving extended active duty, you are considered on extended active duty even if you do not serve more than 90 days.

**Line 10.** To claim the EIC, either you (or your spouse if filing jointly) must be at least age 25 but under age 65 at year-end.

If neither you (nor your spouse if filing jointly) were at least age 25 but under age 65 at year-end on line 1, you cannot claim the EIC. See Publication 596, *Earned Income Credit*.

If your spouse died during the year on line 1, or you are preparing a return for someone who died that year, the age test is met if your spouse or the deceased taxpayer met the applicable minimum age at death.

Your spouse or the deceased taxpayer is considered to reach the minimum age the day before their birthday. However, reaching age 65 occurs on their 65th birthday.

**Example 1:** You are married filing jointly for 2025 with your spouse who died in August 2025. You are age 67. Your spouse would have become age 65 in November 2025. Because your spouse was under age 65 at death, you meet the age test.

**Example 2:** Your spouse was born February 14, 2000, and died February 13, 2025. Your spouse is considered age 25 at death. However, if your spouse died February 12, 2025, your spouse is not considered age 25 at death and is not at least age 25 at year-end 2025.

#### Part III — Child Tax Credit / Additional Child Tax Credit / Credit for Other Dependents

**Line 14.** Generally, a child must live with you for more than half the year to claim the credit. A child is considered to have lived with you for more than half the year if born or died during the year on line 1 and your home was the child's home for more than half the time the child was alive. Temporary absences for special circumstances (school, vacation, business, medical care, military service, or juvenile detention) count as time the child lived with you.

However, exceptions exist for kidnapped children and children of divorced or separated parents. If you meet one of these exceptions, answer "Yes" to question 14 for that child. See your tax return instructions for details.

**Line 15.** A qualifying child for CTC and ACTC purposes is a child who:

1. Is your son, daughter, stepchild, foster child, brother, sister, stepbrother, stepsister, half-brother, half-sister, or a descendant of any of them (for example, grandchild, niece, or nephew).
2. Was under age 17 at year-end.
3. Did not provide over half their own support for the year.
4. Lived with you for more than half the year.
5. Is claimed as a dependent on your return.
6. Does not file a joint return for the year (or files it only to claim a refund of withheld income tax or estimated tax paid).
7. Was a U.S. citizen, U.S. national, or U.S. resident alien. See Publication 519 for definitions.

**Adopted Child:** An adopted child is always treated as your own child. This includes a child lawfully placed with you for legal adoption.

See your tax return instructions for more information on qualifying children.

**Line 16.** You cannot claim the CTC/ACTC/ODC for a person who is not your dependent. See your tax return instructions for more information.

---

#### Part IV — American Opportunity Tax Credit

**Line 19a.** An eligible student for AOTC purposes is a student for whom you pay qualified education expenses and who meets all requirements below:

1. The student did not have expenses used to figure an AOTC in any 4 earlier tax years. This includes any year(s) when the Hope scholarship credit was claimed for the same student.
2. The student had not completed the first 4 years of post-secondary education (generally freshman, sophomore, junior, and senior college years) before the year on line 1.
3. For at least one academic period beginning in the year on line 1 (or the first 3 months of the following year if qualified expenses were paid the previous year), the student was enrolled at least half-time in a program leading to a degree, certificate, or other recognized educational credential.
4. The student has not been convicted of any federal or state felony for possessing or distributing a controlled substance as of year-end on line 1.

See Publication 970 and Form 8863 Instructions for more information.

**Line 19b.** If the AOTC has been claimed for a student for any 4 tax years before the year on line 1 (including any year when the Hope scholarship credit was claimed), the AOTC cannot be claimed for this student for the year on line 1.

You cannot claim the AOTC based on qualified education expenses paid by someone other than yourself or your spouse, unless you are claiming the student as a dependent.
